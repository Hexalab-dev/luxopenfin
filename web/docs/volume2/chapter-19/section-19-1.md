# 19.1 Calculate a Break-Even Point in Units and Dollars

### Understanding Cost Behavior

Before calculating break-even points, we must understand how costs behave.

#### Variable Costs

**Variable costs** are costs that change in total in direct proportion to changes in activity level (volume).

**Characteristics:**
- Total variable costs increase as volume increases
- Total variable costs decrease as volume decreases
- Variable cost per unit remains constant
- Examples: Direct materials, direct labor (if paid per unit), sales commissions

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant uses €5 of ingredients per meal served.
- 100 meals: €5 × 100 = €500 total variable cost
- 200 meals: €5 × 200 = €1,000 total variable cost
- Variable cost per meal: €5 (constant)

**Formula:**
**Total Variable Costs = Variable Cost per Unit × Number of Units**

#### Fixed Costs

**Fixed costs** are costs that remain constant in total regardless of changes in activity level (volume).

**Characteristics:**
- Total fixed costs remain constant as volume changes
- Fixed cost per unit decreases as volume increases
- Fixed cost per unit increases as volume decreases
- Examples: Rent, salaries (fixed), insurance, depreciation

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant pays €3,000 per month in rent.
- 100 meals: €3,000 total fixed cost (€30 per meal)
- 200 meals: €3,000 total fixed cost (€15 per meal)
- 500 meals: €3,000 total fixed cost (€6 per meal)
- Total fixed cost: €3,000 (constant)

**Formula:**
**Total Fixed Costs = Constant Amount (regardless of volume)**

#### Mixed Costs

**Mixed costs** (also called semi-variable costs) have both fixed and variable components.

**Characteristics:**
- Part fixed, part variable
- Total cost changes with volume, but not proportionally
- Examples: Utilities (base charge + usage), phone (monthly fee + per-minute charges)

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant has electricity costs: €200 base charge + €0.50 per meal.
- 100 meals: €200 + (€0.50 × 100) = €250
- 200 meals: €200 + (€0.50 × 200) = €300
- 500 meals: €200 + (€0.50 × 500) = €450

**Formula:**
**Total Mixed Costs = Fixed Component + (Variable Rate × Volume)**

### Contribution Margin

**Contribution margin** is the amount remaining from sales revenue after variable expenses have been deducted. It represents the amount available to cover fixed costs and provide profit.

**Contribution Margin per Unit:**
**Contribution Margin per Unit = Selling Price per Unit - Variable Cost per Unit**

**Total Contribution Margin:**
**Total Contribution Margin = Total Sales Revenue - Total Variable Costs**

**Contribution Margin Ratio:**
**Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution Margin per Unit ÷ Selling Price per Unit**
or
**Contribution Margin Ratio = Total Contribution Margin ÷ Total Sales Revenue**

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant sells meals for €20 each. Variable cost per meal is €8.
- Selling price per unit: €20
- Variable cost per unit: €8
- Contribution margin per unit: €20 - €8 = €12
- Contribution margin ratio: €12 ÷ €20 = 0.60 or 60%

This means for every €1 of sales, €0.60 is available to cover fixed costs and profit.

### Break-Even Point

The **break-even point** is the level of sales at which total revenue equals total costs, resulting in zero profit (or zero loss).

**At Break-Even:**
- Total Revenue = Total Costs
- Profit = €0
- Contribution Margin = Fixed Costs

### Calculating Break-Even Point in Units

**Formula:**
**Break-Even Point (Units) = Total Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit**

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant has:
- Fixed costs: €6,000 per month
- Selling price per meal: €20
- Variable cost per meal: €8
- Contribution margin per meal: €20 - €8 = €12

**Break-Even Point (Units) = €6,000 ÷ €12 = 500 meals per month**

Marie must sell 500 meals per month to break even.

**Verification:**
- Revenue: 500 meals × €20 = €10,000
- Variable costs: 500 meals × €8 = €4,000
- Contribution margin: €10,000 - €4,000 = €6,000
- Fixed costs: €6,000
- Profit: €6,000 - €6,000 = €0 ✓

### Calculating Break-Even Point in Dollars

**Formula:**
**Break-Even Point (Dollars) = Total Fixed Costs ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio**

or

**Break-Even Point (Dollars) = Break-Even Point (Units) × Selling Price per Unit**

**Example:**
Using the same data:
- Fixed costs: €6,000 per month
- Contribution margin ratio: 60% (0.60)

**Break-Even Point (Dollars) = €6,000 ÷ 0.60 = €10,000 per month**

or

**Break-Even Point (Dollars) = 500 meals × €20 = €10,000 per month**

Marie must generate €10,000 in revenue per month to break even.

### Break-Even Analysis Graph

A break-even graph visually shows the relationship between costs, revenue, and volume:

**Graph Components:**
- X-axis: Volume (units sold)
- Y-axis: Costs and Revenue (euros)
- Fixed Cost Line: Horizontal line at fixed cost level
- Total Cost Line: Starts at fixed costs, slopes upward (fixed + variable)
- Revenue Line: Starts at origin, slopes upward (price × volume)
- Break-Even Point: Intersection of revenue and total cost lines

**Graph Interpretation:**
- Area below break-even: Loss zone
- Area above break-even: Profit zone
- Distance to break-even: Safety margin

### Target Profit Analysis

**Target profit** is the desired level of profit. We can calculate the sales volume needed to achieve a target profit.

**Formula:**
**Sales Volume (Units) = (Fixed Costs + Target Profit) ÷ Contribution Margin per Unit**

**Example:**
Marie wants to earn €3,000 profit per month.
- Fixed costs: €6,000
- Target profit: €3,000
- Contribution margin per unit: €12

**Sales Volume (Units) = (€6,000 + €3,000) ÷ €12 = 750 meals per month**

Marie must sell 750 meals per month to earn €3,000 profit.

**Verification:**
- Revenue: 750 meals × €20 = €15,000
- Variable costs: 750 meals × €8 = €6,000
- Contribution margin: €15,000 - €6,000 = €9,000
- Fixed costs: €6,000
- Profit: €9,000 - €6,000 = €3,000 ✓

### Margin of Safety

**Margin of safety** is the amount by which actual or expected sales exceed break-even sales. It indicates how much sales can drop before the business incurs a loss.

**Formula:**
**Margin of Safety (Units) = Actual Sales (Units) - Break-Even Sales (Units)**

**Margin of Safety (Dollars) = Actual Sales (Dollars) - Break-Even Sales (Dollars)**

**Margin of Safety Ratio = Margin of Safety ÷ Actual Sales**

**Example:**
Marie's restaurant sells 800 meals per month. Break-even is 500 meals.
- Actual sales: 800 meals
- Break-even sales: 500 meals
- Margin of safety: 800 - 500 = 300 meals
- Margin of safety (dollars): (800 × €20) - (500 × €20) = €6,000
- Margin of safety ratio: 300 ÷ 800 = 37.5%

Marie can sell 300 fewer meals (or €6,000 less revenue) before incurring a loss.

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

When calculating break-even in Luxembourg:
- Consider VAT in pricing (selling price may include or exclude VAT)
- Fixed costs may include VAT (if not recoverable)
- Variable costs should exclude recoverable VAT
- Break-even analysis helps with VAT planning
- Consider Luxembourg-specific costs (social charges, etc.)
