# Chapter 14: Corporation Accounting

## Chapter Introduction

Marie's restaurant has been so successful that she's considering incorporating. "I want to form a SARL to protect my personal assets and maybe bring in investors," she tells Monsieur Schneider. "But I don't understand how corporate accounting works. How is it different from what I'm doing now?"

Monsieur Schneider explains that corporations have different accounting requirements than sole proprietorships. "Corporations have share capital, retained earnings, and may pay dividends. In Luxembourg, there are specific corporate forms—SA, SARL, SCS, SNC—each with different accounting requirements and PCN classifications." (Equity was introduced in Chapter 2 as part of the accounting equation. This chapter provides detailed coverage of corporate equity structure, building on the equity concepts from earlier chapters. Chapter 15 covers partnership accounting, which has different equity requirements.)

**Corporations** are legal entities separate from their owners. Corporate accounting involves:
- Share capital (equity from owners)
- Retained earnings (accumulated profits)
- Dividends (distributions to owners)
- Different equity structure than sole proprietorships

In Luxembourg, corporate accounting is important because:
- Many SMEs are corporations (SARL, SA)
- Different corporate forms have different requirements
- Share capital must be properly accounted for (PCN Class 1)
- Dividend distributions have specific rules
- Proper accounting ensures compliance

This chapter teaches you about corporation accounting, share capital, retained earnings, dividends, and Luxembourg-specific requirements including corporate forms (SA, SARL, SCS, SNC) and PCN Class 1 share capital accounting.

By the end of this chapter, you'll understand how to properly account for corporations and comply with Luxembourg requirements—just like Marie will learn to do if she incorporates her restaurant.

## 14.1 Explain the Process of Securing Equity Financing through the Issuance of Stock

### Equity Financing

**Equity Financing:**
- Raising capital by selling ownership interests
- Issuing shares to investors
- Investors become owners (shareholders)
- No repayment required (unlike debt)
- Investors share in profits and losses

**Advantages:**
- No repayment obligation
- No interest payments
- Share risk with investors
- Can raise significant capital

**Figure 14.1:** Corporation Equity Structure

```
                    Corporation Equity
                           │
        ┌──────────────────┼──────────────────┐
        │                  │                  │
   Share Capital      Retained Earnings    Other Equity
        │                  │                  │
   ┌────┴────┐        ┌────┴────┐        ┌────┴────┐
   │         │        │         │        │         │
Common    Preferred  Beginning  Net      Treasury  Other
Stock     Stock      Balance    Income    Stock    Equity
```

*Alt text: Diagram showing the structure of corporation equity divided into three main components: Share Capital (Common Stock, Preferred Stock), Retained Earnings (Beginning Balance, Net Income), and Other Equity (Treasury Stock, Other Equity), demonstrating how corporate equity is organized.*
- Investors may provide expertise

**Disadvantages:**
- Dilutes ownership
- Share profits with investors
- May lose control
- More complex than debt
- Must comply with corporate law

### Share Capital

**Share Capital:**
- Amount invested by owners
- Recorded at par value or issue price
- Represents ownership stake
- Cannot be withdrawn (unlike loans)
- Permanent capital

**Par Value:**
- Nominal value per share
- May be €1, €10, or other amount
- Set in corporate documents
- May differ from market value

**Issue Price:**
- Price at which shares are sold
- May equal par value
- May exceed par value (premium)
- Determined by market or agreement

### Issuance of Stock

**Process:**
1. Company decides to issue shares
2. Sets price per share
3. Sells shares to investors
4. Receives cash
5. Records share capital
6. Updates share register
7. Files with RCS (if required)

**Example:**
- Issue 1,000 shares at €10 per share
- Receive €10,000 cash

**Journal Entry:**
```
5131   Cash                       €10,000
101    Share Capital                          €10,000
To record issuance of shares
```

**PCN Account:**
- **101**: Share Capital (Capital Social)

**Share Register:**
- Must maintain list of shareholders
- Track share ownership
- Record transfers
- Required for compliance

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

Share issuance in Luxembourg:
- Must comply with corporate law
- Must use proper PCN accounts (Class 1)
- Must maintain share register
- Must file with RCS
- Must follow corporate form requirements
- Minimum capital requirements apply
- Must be properly documented

## 14.2 Analyze and Record Transactions for the Issuance and Repurchase of Stock

### Stock Issuance

**At Par Value:**
- Issue price = Par value
- Record at par value

**Above Par Value:**
- Issue price > Par value
- Par value to Share Capital
- Excess to Share Premium

**Example:**
- Issue 1,000 shares, par value €1, issue price €10
- Par value: €1,000
- Premium: €9,000

**Journal Entry:**
```
5131   Cash                       €10,000
101    Share Capital                          €1,000
111    Share Premium                           €9,000
To record share issuance above par
```

**PCN Accounts:**
- **101**: Share Capital
- **111**: Share Premium (Prime d'Émission)

### Stock Repurchase

**Treasury Stock:**
- Company buys back its own shares
- Reduces outstanding shares
- Recorded as contra-equity

**Example:**
- Repurchase 100 shares at €12 per share

**Journal Entry:**
```
502    Treasury Stock             €1,200
5131   Cash                                   €1,200
To record share repurchase
```

**PCN Account:**
- **502**: Treasury Stock (Actions Propres)

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

Stock transactions in Luxembourg:
- Must comply with corporate law
- Must use proper PCN accounts
- Must maintain records
- Must file with RCS
- Must follow form-specific rules

## 14.3 Record Transactions and the Effects on Financial Statements for Cash Dividends, Property Dividends, Stock Dividends, and Stock Splits

### Cash Dividends

**Cash Dividends:**
- Distribution of cash to shareholders
- Reduce retained earnings
- Create liability when declared

**Declaration:**
```
141    Retained Earnings          €5,000
4712   Dividends Payable                      €5,000
To declare cash dividend
```

**Payment:**
```
4712   Dividends Payable          €5,000
5131   Cash                                   €5,000
To pay cash dividend
```

### Stock Dividends

**Stock Dividends:**
- Distribution of additional shares
- No cash paid
- Reduces retained earnings
- Increases share capital

**Example:**
- 10% stock dividend on 10,000 shares
- Issue 1,000 new shares

**Journal Entry:**
```
141    Retained Earnings          €[amount]
101    Share Capital                           €[amount]
To record stock dividend
```

### Stock Splits

**Stock Splits:**
- Increase number of shares
- Decrease par value proportionally
- No journal entry (just memo)
- No change in total equity

**Example:**
- 2-for-1 stock split
- 10,000 shares @ €10 par → 20,000 shares @ €5 par
- No journal entry needed

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

Dividends in Luxembourg:
- Must comply with corporate law
- Must have sufficient retained earnings
- Must follow distribution rules
- Must use proper PCN accounts
- Tax implications apply

## 14.4 Compare and Contrast Owners' Equity versus Retained Earnings

### Owners' Equity

**Owners' Equity Components:**
- Share Capital
- Share Premium
- Retained Earnings
- Other Reserves

**Total Equity:**
**Total Equity = Share Capital + Premium + Retained Earnings + Reserves**

### Retained Earnings

**Retained Earnings:**
- Accumulated profits not distributed
- Increases with net income
- Decreases with net loss and dividends
- Part of total equity

**Formula:**
**Ending Retained Earnings = Beginning + Net Income - Dividends**

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

Equity in Luxembourg:
- Must be properly classified (PCN Class 1)
- Must maintain accurate records
- Must comply with corporate law
- Must support all balances

## 14.5 Discuss the Applicability of Earnings per Share as a Method to Measure Performance

### Earnings per Share (EPS)

**EPS Formula:**
**EPS = Net Income ÷ Weighted Average Shares Outstanding**

**Purpose:**
- Measure profitability per share
- Compare across companies
- Important for investors

**Example:**
- Net Income: €50,000
- Shares Outstanding: 10,000
- EPS: €50,000 ÷ 10,000 = €5.00 per share

### Luxembourg Considerations

**EPS in Luxembourg:**
- Required for public companies
- Optional for private companies
- Useful for performance measurement
- Can be calculated for any corporation

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

EPS in Luxembourg:
- Required disclosure for some companies
- Must be calculated correctly
- Must be disclosed if material
- Useful for analysis

## 14.6 Luxembourg Corporate Forms (SA, SARL, SCS, SNC)

### Corporate Forms in Luxembourg

**SA (Société Anonyme):**
- Public limited company
- Minimum capital: €30,000
- Shares freely transferable
- More formal requirements

**SARL (Société à Responsabilité Limitée):**
- Private limited company
- Minimum capital: €12,000
- Most common for SMEs
- Shares not freely transferable

**SCS (Société en Commandite Simple):**
- Limited partnership
- General and limited partners
- Different liability

**SNC (Société en Nom Collectif):**
- General partnership
- All partners liable
- Less common

### Accounting Implications

**Each Form Has:**
- Different capital requirements
- Different accounting requirements
- Different PCN classifications
- Different filing requirements

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

Corporate forms in Luxembourg:
- Must comply with form-specific law
- Must meet capital requirements
- Must use proper PCN accounts
- Must file with RCS
- Must follow accounting standards

## 14.7 Luxembourg Share Capital Accounting (PCN Class 1)

### PCN Class 1: Financement Permanent

**Class 1** contains equity and long-term financing.

**Account Ranges:**
- 10–13: Share Capital (Capital Social)
- 13: Reserves (Réserves)
- 19: Long-Term Debt

### Share Capital Accounts

**Main Accounts:**
- **101**: Share Capital (Capital Social)
- **111**: Share Premium (Prime d'Émission)
- **131**: Legal Reserve (Réserve Légale)
- **138**: Other Reserves (Autres Réserves)
- **141**: Retained Earnings (Résultat Reporté)

### Luxembourg Compliance Note

PCN Class 1 requirements:
- Must use proper account classifications
- Must maintain accurate records
- Must comply with PCN structure
- Must support all balances
- Must enable proper reporting

   a) Loans from banks

2. Which PCN account is used for share capital?
a) 44111
b) 101

   **Answer:** b) 101 is Share Capital (Capital Social).

3. Cash dividends:
   a) Increase retained earnings
