5.3 Apply the Results from the Adjusted Trial Balance to Compute Current Ratio and Working Capital Balance, and Explain How These Measures Represent Liquidity
Understanding Liquidity
  • Indicates financial health
  • Shows ability to pay bills
  • Important to creditors and investors
  • Helps with cash flow management
Current Ratio
  • > 1.0: Business has more current assets than current liabilities (good)
  • = 1.0: Current assets equal current liabilities (adequate)
  • < 1.0: Business has fewer current assets than current liabilities (potential problem)
  • > 1.5: Good liquidity
  • 1.0 - 1.5: Acceptable liquidity
  • < 1.0: Poor liquidity (may struggle to pay bills)

5.6 Luxembourg Annual Closing and RCS Filing Requirements
Annual Closing Process
Annual Closing Timeline
  • Year End (Dec 31): Last day of fiscal year
  • Within 7 Months (July 31): Annual accounts must be filed with RCS
  • December 31 (following year): Corporate tax return (modèle 500) due
  • Before March 1: Annual VAT return due (annual-only filers, turnover up to €112,000); before May 1 for monthly/quarterly filers
Key Terms
Closing Entries
Journal entries that transfer temporary account balances to permanent accounts
Current Ratio
Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities; measures liquidity
Income Summary
Temporary account used in closing process to summarize revenues and expenses
Liquidity
Ability to meet short-term obligations using current assets
Permanent Accounts
Accounts that carry balances forward (assets, liabilities, equity)
Key Terms (continued)
Post-Closing Trial Balance
Trial balance prepared after closing entries, contains only permanent accounts
Temporary Accounts
Accounts closed at period end (revenues, expenses)
Working Capital
Current Assets - Current Liabilities; excess of current assets over current liabilities
