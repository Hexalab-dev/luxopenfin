25.2 Evaluate the Payback and Accounting Rate of Return in Capital Investment Decisions
Payback Period
  • Accumulate cash inflows until they equal the initial investment
  • Initial investment: €150,000
  • Annual cash inflow: €50,000
  • Payback: €150,000 ÷ €50,000 = 3 years
  • Initial investment: €200,000
  • Year 1: €60,000
Discounted Payback Period
  • Initial investment: €200,000
  • Discount rate: 8%
  • Cash inflows: €60,000, €70,000, €80,000, €90,000
  • Discount each inflow: Year 1 PV = €60,000 / (1.08)^1 = €55,556
  • Accumulate PVs until payback achieved
25.3 Explain the Time Value of Money and Calculate Present and Future Values of Lump Sums and Annuities
Time Value of Money (TVM)
Future Value (FV)
  • PV = present value
  • r = interest rate per period
  • n = number of periods
  • Invest €10,000 at 5% for 3 years
  • FV = €10,000 × (1.05)^3 = €11,576.25
25.4 Use Discounted Cash Flow Models to Make Capital Investment Decisions
Net Present Value (NPV)
  • Ct = cash flow at time t
  • r = discount rate
  • C0 = initial investment
  • NPV > 0: Accept project
  • NPV < 0: Reject project
  • Initial investment: €250,000
Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
  • IRR > required rate of return: Accept
  • IRR < required rate of return: Reject
  • Use Excel IRR function or financial calculator
  • Considers time value of money
  • Provides rate of return
  • Can have multiple IRRs (non-conventional cash flows)
25.5 Compare and Contrast Non-Time-Value-Based Methods and Time-Value-Based Methods
Non-Time-Value-Based Methods
  • Focus: Liquidity
  • Pros: Simple, highlights risk
  • Cons: Ignores time value and cash flows after payback
  • Accounts for time value but still ignores post-payback cash flows
  • Focus: Accounting profitability
  • Pros: Uses familiar accounting data
Time-Value-Based Methods
  • Focus: Value creation
  • Pros: Considers time value, cash flows, risk
  • Cons: Requires accurate discount rate
  • Focus: Rate of return
  • Pros: Intuitive, considers time value
  • Cons: Multiple IRRs possible, reinvestment assumption
25.6 Determine and Evaluate Payback Periods and Discounted Payback
Payback in Practice
  • Initial investment: €350,000
  • Cash inflows: €90k, €100k, €110k, €120k, €130k
  • Payback: 3.4 years
  • If maximum acceptable payback = 4 years → Acceptable
Discounted Payback Example
  • Discount rate: 8%
  • Discounted inflows:
  • Year 1: €90k / 1.08 = €83,333
  • Year 2: €100k / 1.08^2 = €85,733
  • Year 3: €110k / 1.08^3 = €87,032
  • Year 4: €120k / 1.08^4 = €88,073

25.8 Understand Luxembourg SME Investment Decision Making
Cost of Capital for Luxembourg SMEs
  • Bank loans (fixed or variable rates)
  • Government-backed loans (SNCI, House of Entrepreneurship)
  • Equity injections from owners or investors
  • Leasing (equipment, vehicles)
  • Cost of debt: Bank lending rate × (1 - tax rate)
  • Cost of equity: Owner's required return (risk-based)
Financing Structure
  • Use conservative leverage (debt ≦ 60%) to manage risk
  • Consider covenants from banks (debt service coverage ratio)
  • Evaluate leasing vs. buying (NPV of lease vs. purchase)
25.9 Evaluate Luxembourg Government Grants and Investment Incentives
Government Support Programs
  • Luxinnovation: Support for innovation, digitalization, R&D
  • House of Entrepreneurship: Guidance, grants, financing support
  • SNCI (Société Nationale de Crédit et d'Investissement): Loans, quasi-equity
  • Ministry of the Economy: Investment aid (regional, environmental, tourism)
  • Chamber of Commerce: Training, subsidies
