24.1 Describe the Balanced Scorecard Framework
What Is the Balanced Scorecard?
  • Focuses on four perspectives: Financial, Customer, Internal Processes, Learning & Growth
  • Connects long-term strategy to daily operations
  • Balances lagging indicators (financial) with leading indicators (non-financial)
  • Provides a framework for strategy execution
  • Encourages continuous improvement
Four Perspectives of the Balanced Scorecard
  • Measures profitability, growth, shareholder value
  • Lagging indicators (results of past efforts)
  • Measures customer satisfaction, loyalty, market share
  • Leading indicators that drive financial results
  • Measures process efficiency, quality, innovation
  • Ensures operations support strategy
24.2 Explain the Four Perspectives of the Balanced Scorecard
1. Financial Perspective
  • Increase revenue
  • Improve profitability
  • Enhance cash flow
  • Manage risk
  • Increase shareholder value
  • Revenue growth rate
2. Customer Perspective
  • Improve customer satisfaction
  • Increase customer loyalty
  • Expand market share
  • Improve brand reputation
  • Enhance customer experience
  • Customer satisfaction score (CSAT)

Aligning Objectives with Strategy
Strategy Example: Le Petit Bistro
  • Customer experience
  • Operational excellence
  • Sustainable growth
  • Increase revenue from catering by 20%
  • Improve profit margin by 5%

24.4 Select and Define Performance Measures for Each Perspective
Selecting Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
  • Align with objectives
  • Are measurable and reliable
  • Provide insight (leading indicators)
  • Are actionable (managers can influence)
  • Are balanced (not too many metrics)

24.5 Integrate Balanced Scorecard Metrics with Budgeting and Variance Analysis
Linking BSC to Budgeting
Balanced Scorecard Variance Analysis
  • Customer satisfaction target: 90%
  • Actual: 85%
  • Variance: -5% (unfavorable)
  • Investigate causes (service issues, quality)
24.6 Describe How Companies Use Variance Analysis Beyond Financial Metrics
Extending Variance Analysis
  • Customer satisfaction variance (target vs. actual)
  • Process efficiency variance (e.g., cycle time)
  • Quality variance (defect rate vs. target)
  • Learning variance (training hours vs. target)
Non-Financial Variance Example
  • Target: 90%
  • Actual: 85%
  • Variance: -5% (unfavorable)
  • Investigate causes: Service delays, quality issues, staff attitude
  • Action: Training, process improvement, more staffing
24.7 Use Non-Financial Performance Measures to Improve Decision-Making
Non-Financial Metrics
  • Customer feedback
  • Employee engagement
  • Quality indicators
  • Innovation metrics
  • Compliance metrics
Leading Indicators
  • Customer satisfaction predicts future sales
  • Employee engagement predicts retention
  • Process quality predicts rework costs
24.8 Apply Balanced Scorecard Concepts to Luxembourg SMEs
Balanced Scorecard Implementation Steps
  • Clarify mission and strategic goals
  • Identify strategic themes (customer focus, innovation, efficiency)
  • Link objectives across four perspectives
  • Show cause-and-effect relationships
  • Choose relevant metrics for each objective
  • Set targets and define data sources
Balanced Scorecard Example: Luxembourg Trust and Finance Firm
  • Objectives: Increase assets under management, improve profitability
  • KPIs: Assets under management (AUM), fee income growth, cost-to-income ratio
  • Objectives: Improve client satisfaction, expand international clientele
  • KPIs: Client satisfaction score, client retention rate, number of new clients
  • Objectives: Improve compliance, reduce onboarding time
  • KPIs: Compliance audit results, onboarding cycle time, error rate
24.9 Evaluate the Benefits and Challenges of Implementing a Balanced Scorecard
Benefits
  • Aligns strategy and operations
  • Balances short-term and long-term priorities
  • Improves communication and understanding of strategy
  • Provides comprehensive performance view
  • Supports decision-making with data
  • Encourages continuous improvement
Challenges
  • Requires cultural change and management commitment
  • Needs clear strategy and objectives
  • Requires data collection and IT systems
  • Potential for metric overload
  • Must be maintained and updated
