22.1 Explain Why and How Activity-Based Costing Is Used
Limitations of Traditional Costing
  • Overcosting: Some products are assigned too much overhead
  • Undercosting: Some products are assigned too little overhead
  • Single Allocation Base: May not reflect actual cost drivers
  • Volume Bias: High-volume products may subsidize low-volume products
  • Inaccurate Profitability: May show unprofitable products as profitable
Example: Traditional Costing Problem
  • Total overhead: €30,000 per month
  • Total direct labor hours: 2,000 hours
  • Overhead rate: €30,000 ÷ 2,000 = €15 per direct labor hour
  • Simple Dish: Takes 0.5 hours, uses simple equipment
  • Complex Dish: Takes 1.0 hour, uses specialized equipment, requires setup time
  • Simple Dish overhead: 0.5 × €15 = €7.50

22.3 Compare and Contrast Traditional and Activity-Based Costing Systems
Traditional Costing System
  • Single overhead rate
  • Single allocation base (usually direct labor)
  • Simple to implement
  • Less accurate for diverse products
  • Volume-based allocation
  • Total overhead: €30,000
Activity-Based Costing System
  • Multiple overhead rates
  • Multiple allocation bases (cost drivers)
  • More complex to implement
  • More accurate for diverse products
  • Activity-based allocation
  • Setup activity: €5,000 ÷ 100 setups = €50 per setup
22.4 Compare and Contrast Traditional and Activity-Based Costing Methods
Detailed Comparison
Cost Allocation Philosophy
  • Assumes overhead is driven by volume (direct labor, machine hours)
  • "All products consume overhead proportionally to volume"
  • Simple assumption
  • Assumes overhead is driven by activities
  • "Different products consume different activities"
  • More realistic assumption
22.5 Explain and Identify Cost Pools and Cost Drivers
Cost Pools
  • Group related costs together
  • Simplify cost allocation
  • Organize overhead costs
  • Setup cost pool
  • Inspection cost pool
  • Ordering cost pool
Creating Cost Pools
  • Setup activity: Setup labor €3,000 + Setup materials €500 + Setup equipment €1,500 = €5,000 cost pool
  • Processing activity: Processing labor €10,000 + Equipment depreciation €3,000 + Utilities €2,000 = €15,000 cost pool
22.6 Luxembourg SME Activity-Based Costing Applications
Luxembourg SME Characteristics
  • Service companies with diverse services
  • Manufacturing with multiple products
  • Businesses with significant overhead
  • Companies with complex operations
  • Businesses needing accurate cost information
Example 1: Luxembourg Service Company
  • Client acquisition: €10,000 (cost driver: number of new clients)
  • Project setup: €8,000 (cost driver: number of projects)
  • Research: €12,000 (cost driver: research hours)
  • Client meetings: €6,000 (cost driver: meeting hours)
  • Report preparation: €4,000 (cost driver: number of reports)
  • Total overhead: €40,000
Key Terms
Activity-Based Costing (ABC)
Costing method that allocates overhead based on activities
Activity
Task or action that consumes resources
Cost Pool
Grouping of costs by activity
Cost Driver
Factor that causes or drives activity cost
Activity Rate
Cost per unit of cost driver
Key Terms (continued)
Traditional Costing
Costing using single overhead rate
Overcosting
Assigning too much cost to a product
Undercosting
Assigning too little cost to a product
Volume Bias
Tendency to overcost high-volume products
Resource Consumption
Amount of resources used by a product
Key Terms (continued)
Activity Consumption
Amount of activities used by a product
