# Solutions Manual - Chapter 11: Long-Term Assets

## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: Tangible vs. Intangible Assets
**Answer:**
- **Tangible assets** are physical resources such as buildings, machinery, vehicles, and equipment that can be touched and seen. They are recorded in PCN group 22 (tangible fixed assets) and depreciated over their useful lives.
- **Intangible assets** lack physical substance but provide future economic benefits, such as patents, licenses, software, trademarks, and goodwill. They are recorded in PCN group 21 (e.g., 212 “Concessions, patents, licences, trademarks”; 213 goodwill) and amortized.
- **Examples:**
  - Tangible: Restaurant ovens (222 Installations techniques et machines), delivery vans (2232 Véhicules de transport).
  - Intangible: Software licence (21213), acquired trademark (212).

### Question 2: Capitalize vs. Expense
**Answer:**
- **Capitalize** costs that provide benefits beyond the current period, extend the asset’s life, increase capacity, or are necessary to put the asset into service (purchase price, import duties, delivery, installation, testing, professional fees). These become part of the asset’s book value and are depreciated.
- **Expense** costs that maintain current condition, are routine/recurring, or relate to general operations (minor repairs, routine maintenance, consumables). They impact the income statement immediately.
- **Decision factors:** useful life extension, increased productivity, substantial cost relative to capitalization threshold, regulatory requirements, materiality.

### Question 3: Straight-Line vs. Declining Balance Depreciation
**Answer:**
- **Straight-Line (SL):** Allocates equal depreciation each year: (Cost − Salvage) ÷ Useful Life. Appropriate for assets that provide uniform benefits (buildings, furniture). Straightforward, stable expense recognition.
- **Declining Balance (DB/DDB):** Accelerated method applying a fixed rate to book value (e.g., double-declining = 2 × SL rate). Higher depreciation in early years, lower later. Useful for assets that lose value quickly or deliver higher early-period benefits (technology, vehicles). Luxembourg tax law often allows accelerated rates for qualifying assets, making DB advantageous for tax planning.

### Question 4: Straight-Line Depreciation Calculation
**Answer:**
1. Gather **cost**, **salvage value**, and **useful life**.
2. Compute **depreciable base** = Cost − Salvage.
3. Calculate **annual depreciation** = Depreciable Base ÷ Useful Life.
4. Record periodic journal entry: Debit 633 “DCV sur immobilisations corporelles” (Depreciation Expense) / Credit the accumulated depreciation sub-account of the asset (e.g., 2229 for equipment 222).
5. Pro-rate for partial years when necessary.

### Question 5: Amortization vs. Depreciation
**Answer:**
- **Depreciation**: allocation of tangible asset cost over useful life.
- **Amortization**: allocation of intangible asset cost (finite life). Uses similar straight-line approach unless another pattern better reflects consumption.
- **Key differences:** Asset type, account numbers (expense 633 for tangible depreciation vs. 632 for intangible amortisation; accumulated sub-accounts such as 2229 vs. 2129), possible indefinite-life intangibles (goodwill) not amortized but tested for impairment.

### Question 6: PCN Class 2 Accounts
**Answer:**
Key Luxembourg PCN Class 2 accounts include:
- 201 “Frais de constitution” (formation expenses, group 20)
- 212 “Concessions, brevets, licences, marques” (patents, software licences 21213); 213 goodwill (group 21)
- 2211 “Terrains” (land); 2213 “Constructions” (buildings)
- 222 “Installations techniques et machines” (equipment)
- 2232 “Véhicules de transport”; 2234 furniture; 2235 IT hardware
- 231 / 233 / 235 financial fixed assets (group 23)
- The PCN has no separate class for accumulated depreciation: this textbook opens a sub-account ending in 9 under each asset (e.g., 2229 equipment, 22329 vehicles, 22139 buildings, 2129 intangibles).

### Question 7: Goodwill Treatment
**Answer:**
- **Goodwill** arises from business combinations when purchase price exceeds fair value of identifiable net assets. Recorded under 213 (goodwill acquired for valuable consideration).
- Not amortized under Luxembourg GAAP if indefinite life; instead, subject to annual impairment testing or amortized over a justified period (often ≤10 years) if reliable impairment testing is not feasible.
- Cannot be internally generated; only recognized when acquired.

### Question 8: Impairment
**Answer:**
- **Impairment** occurs when asset’s recoverable amount (higher of fair value less costs to sell and value in use) falls below book value.
- Test when indicators exist: market declines, obsolescence, physical damage, adverse legal/regulatory changes, cash flow underperformance.
- Record impairment loss: Debit 633 (tangible) or 632 (intangible) “Dotations aux corrections de valeur”, Credit the accumulated sub-account (e.g., 2229 / 2129) or directly the asset. (There are no extraordinary items in Lux GAAP since 2015.)

### Question 9: Asset Disposal Accounting
**Answer:**
1. Remove asset cost (credit asset account).
2. Remove accumulated depreciation (debit e.g. 2229).
3. Record proceeds (debit cash 5131 or receivable).
4. Difference → gain (credit 744) or loss (debit 644).
5. Record VAT if sale is taxable (output VAT 461411) unless the asset is exempt.

### Question 10: Luxembourg Tax Depreciation Impact
**Answer:**
- Tax laws may permit accelerated rates, special depreciation, or investment credits. Book depreciation under Luxembourg GAAP (PCN) may differ from tax depreciation.
- Differences create deferred tax effects and require reconciliation in the notes. Companies maintain depreciation tables for both statutory books and tax filings.
- Investment incentives (e.g., super deductions) affect taxable income but not necessarily book depreciation, so management must track timing differences carefully.

## Problems Set A - Solutions

### Problem A-1: Straight-Line Depreciation
Cost €20,000; salvage €2,000; life 10 yrs.
- Depreciable base = €18,000
- Annual depreciation = €18,000 ÷ 10 = **€1,800**
- Entry: Debit 633 €1,800 / Credit 2229 €1,800.

### Problem A-2: Double-Declining Balance
Cost €20,000; life 10 yrs; rate = 2 × (1/10) = 20%.
- Year 1: €20,000 × 20% = **€4,000** (book value €16,000)
- Year 2: €16,000 × 20% = **€3,200** (book value €12,800)
- Year 3: €12,800 × 20% = **€2,560** (book value €10,240)

### Problem A-3: Capitalize vs. Expense
- a) Equipment purchase €5,000 → **Capitalize** (222)
- b) Delivery €200 → **Capitalize** (part of asset cost)
- c) Installation €500 → **Capitalize**
- d) Routine maintenance €100 → **Expense** (6122)
- e) Major renovation extending life €3,000 → **Capitalize** (add to the asset account, e.g. 222 or 2213)

### Problem A-4: Asset Disposal Entry
Cost €15,000; accumulated depreciation €12,000; sale €4,000.
- Book value = €3,000 → Gain €1,000.
- Entry:
  - Debit 5131 Cash €4,000
  - Debit 2229 Accumulated Depreciation €12,000
  - Credit 222 Equipment €15,000
  - Credit 744 Gain on Disposal €1,000

### Problem A-5: Intangible Amortization
Patent €60,000; life 15 yrs.
- Annual amortization = €60,000 ÷ 15 = **€4,000**
- Entry: Debit 632 Amortization Expense €4,000 / Credit 2129 Accumulated Amortization €4,000.

### Problem B-2: Partial Year Depreciation

### Step 2: Depreciation for 2024 (Straight-Line, Pro-Rated)
- Kitchen equipment: Cost €25,000; salvage €2,500; life 10 yrs; base €22,500.

- Vehicle: Cost €30,000; salvage €5,000; life 5 yrs; base €25,000 → Annual €5,000.

## Case Solutions

### Case 11-1: Capitalize or Expense
1. **Capitalized costs:**
   - New kitchen equipment €20,000 (222)
   - Delivery/installation €2,000 (part of asset)
   - New light fixtures €5,000 (if improve ambiance/life) → capitalize to 2213 (building fixtures) or 2238
   - Training cost? Generally expensed unless required to bring asset to intended use; for IFRS/PCN best practice, expense €1,000 (6188) unless training is necessary to operate new specialized machinery (rare).
2. **Expensed costs:**
   - Painting €3,000 (routine maintenance)
   - Routine maintenance €500
   - Staff training €1,000 (see above)
3. **PCN accounts:** 222 equipment, 2213 building improvements, 6122 maintenance and painting, 6188 training, 421611 input VAT, 44111 suppliers.
4. **VAT:** record input VAT on capitalized and expensed invoices; reclaim through 421611. Non-deductible VAT (if partial exemption) would be added to asset cost.
5. **Depreciation:** kitchen equipment and fixtures depreciated over useful life (straight-line or declining balance depending on policy).

### Case 11-2: Choosing Depreciation Method
1. **Straight-Line Advantages:** simplicity, stable expense, easier budgeting, consistent with IFRS/PCN default. **Disadvantages:** may understate early wear.
   **Declining Balance Advantages:** matches higher early usage, accelerates tax deductions. **Disadvantages:** more complex, lower profits initially.
2. **Tax rules:** Luxembourg allows accelerated depreciation for certain assets (e.g., 2× straight-line) and special amortization for energy-efficient investments. Company may use straight-line in books and accelerate for tax, but must disclose differences.
3. **Financial statement implications:** Straight-line yields steady profit; DB reduces early income but increases later earnings. Balance sheet net asset values differ in early years.
4. **Recommendation:** choose straight-line for financial reporting consistency unless asset clearly benefits from accelerated method and stakeholders accept earnings front-loading. Maintain separate tax depreciation schedule if accelerated deduction desired.
