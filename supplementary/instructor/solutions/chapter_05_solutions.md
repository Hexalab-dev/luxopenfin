# Solutions Manual - Chapter 5: Completing the Accounting Cycle

## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: Purpose of Closing Entries

**Answer:**

**Purpose of Closing Entries:**
Closing entries transfer balances from temporary accounts (revenues and expenses) to permanent accounts (equity/retained earnings) at the end of an accounting period.

**Why They Are Necessary:**

1. **Reset Temporary Accounts:**
   - Revenue and expense accounts are reset to zero for the next period
   - Allows accounts to accumulate new period's transactions from zero
   - Ensures each period's financial statements reflect only that period's activities

2. **Update Retained Earnings:**
   - Net income or loss is transferred to retained earnings
   - Retained earnings reflects cumulative profits/losses
   - Equity is properly updated on the balance sheet

3. **Complete the Accounting Cycle:**
   - Closing is the final step in the accounting cycle
   - Prepares books for the next accounting period
   - Ensures cycle is properly completed

4. **Accurate Period Reporting:**
   - Each period's financial statements show only that period's results
   - Prevents mixing of periods' revenues and expenses
   - Required for accurate financial reporting

5. **Compliance Requirements:**
   - Luxembourg requires proper closing procedures
   - Annual accounts filed with RCS must be properly closed
   - PCN compliance requires proper account treatment

**Example:** Without closing entries, if a business earns €10,000 in January and €12,000 in February, the revenue account would show €22,000 in February, making it impossible to determine February's performance separately.

### Question 2: Four Steps in Closing Process

**Answer:**

The closing process involves four steps:

**Step 1: Close Revenue Accounts to Income Summary**
- **Purpose:** Transfer all revenue account balances to Income Summary
- **Process:** Debit each revenue account for its balance, Credit Income Summary for total revenues
- **Result:** Revenue accounts have zero balances, Income Summary shows total revenues
- **Example:**
```
7061   Sales Revenue              €25,000
142    Income Summary                        €25,000
To close revenue accounts
```

**Step 2: Close Expense Accounts to Income Summary**
- **Purpose:** Transfer all expense account balances to Income Summary
- **Process:** Credit each expense account for its balance, Debit Income Summary for total expenses
- **Result:** Expense accounts have zero balances, Income Summary shows net income or loss
- **Example:**
```
142    Income Summary            €22,000
6211   Salaries Expense                      €15,000
6111   Rent Expense                           €3,000
6184   Utilities Expense                      €1,500
6188   Other Expenses                         €2,500
To close expense accounts
```

**Step 3: Close Income Summary to Retained Earnings**
- **Purpose:** Transfer net income or loss to Retained Earnings
- **Process:** 
  - If net income: Debit Income Summary, Credit Retained Earnings
  - If net loss: Credit Income Summary, Debit Retained Earnings
- **Result:** Income Summary has zero balance, Retained Earnings is updated
- **Example (Net Income):**
```
142    Income Summary            €3,000
141    Retained Earnings                      €3,000
To close income summary (net income)
```

**Step 4: Close Withdrawals/Distributions to Retained Earnings (if applicable)**
- **Purpose:** Transfer owner withdrawals or distributions to Retained Earnings
- **Process:** Credit Withdrawals account, Debit Retained Earnings
- **Result:** Withdrawals account has zero balance, Retained Earnings is reduced
- **Example:**
```
141    Retained Earnings         €1,000
138    Owner Withdrawals                      €1,000
To close withdrawals account
```

**Note:** Step 4 is only needed if there are withdrawals or distributions. Many businesses skip this step if there are no withdrawals.

### Question 3: Temporary vs. Permanent Accounts

**Answer:**

**Temporary Accounts** (Closed at Period End):

**Definition:** Accounts whose balances are reset to zero at the end of each accounting period. They accumulate activity for one period only.

**Examples:**
- **Revenue Accounts (Class 7):**
  - 7061 Sales Revenue
  - 703 Service Revenue
  - 7552 Interest Revenue

- **Expense Accounts (Class 6):**
  - 6211 Salaries Expense
  - 6111 Rent Expense
  - 6184 Utilities Expense
  - 633 Depreciation Expense

- **Income Summary (Class 1):**
  - 142 Income Summary (temporary account used in closing)

**Characteristics:**
- Balances reset to zero after closing
- Accumulate activity for one period
- Appear on Income Statement
- Must be closed at period end

**Permanent Accounts** (Not Closed):

**Definition:** Accounts whose balances carry forward from one period to the next. They represent ongoing balances.

**Examples:**
- **Asset Accounts:**
  - 5131 Cash
  - 4011 Accounts Receivable
  - 361 Inventory
  - 222 Equipment

- **Liability Accounts:**
  - 44111 Accounts Payable
  - 461411 VAT Payable
  - 1942 Bank Loan

- **Equity Accounts:**
  - 101 Share Capital
  - 141 Retained Earnings

**Characteristics:**
- Balances carry forward to next period
- Represent ongoing balances
- Appear on Balance Sheet
- Not closed (but Retained Earnings is updated)

**Key Difference:** Temporary accounts are reset each period, permanent accounts continue with their balances.

### Question 4: Post-Closing Trial Balance

**Answer:**

**Definition:**
A post-closing trial balance is a trial balance prepared after all closing entries have been recorded and posted. It contains only permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, and equity).

**How It Differs from Adjusted Trial Balance:**

| Aspect | Adjusted Trial Balance | Post-Closing Trial Balance |
|--------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| **Timing** | Prepared after adjusting entries | Prepared after closing entries |
| **Accounts Included** | All accounts (permanent and temporary) | Only permanent accounts |
| **Temporary Accounts** | Show balances (revenues, expenses) | Show zero balances |
| **Purpose** | Prepare financial statements | Verify closing, prepare for next period |
| **Income Summary** | Not present | Should be zero (if used) |
| **Retained Earnings** | Beginning balance | Updated balance (after closing) |

**What It Shows:**
- Only permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, equity)
- Updated Retained Earnings (after net income/loss transfer)
- Zero balances for all temporary accounts
- Starting balances for the next period

**Purpose:**
1. Verify closing process completed correctly
2. Confirm temporary accounts are zero
3. Verify mathematical accuracy (debits = credits)
4. Show starting balances for next period
5. Complete the accounting cycle

**Example:**
- Adjusted Trial Balance shows: Sales Revenue €25,000, Salaries Expense €15,000
- Post-Closing Trial Balance shows: Sales Revenue €0, Salaries Expense €0, Retained Earnings updated

### Question 5: Current Ratio and Working Capital

**Answer:**

**Current Ratio:**
- **Formula:** Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
- **Definition:** Measures a business's ability to pay short-term obligations
- **Interpretation:**
  - Ratio > 1.5: Generally indicates good liquidity
  - Ratio = 1.0: Current assets equal current liabilities
  - Ratio < 1.0: May indicate liquidity problems

**Working Capital:**
- **Formula:** Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities
- **Definition:** Amount of current assets available after paying current liabilities
- **Interpretation:**
  - Positive: Business has more current assets than current liabilities (good)
  - Negative: Business has more current liabilities than current assets (concerning)
  - Higher is generally better (more financial flexibility)

**How They Measure Liquidity:**

**Liquidity** is the ability to pay short-term obligations as they come due.

**Current Ratio:**
- Shows relationship between current assets and current liabilities
- Higher ratio = more ability to pay short-term debts
- Indicates financial cushion

**Working Capital:**
- Shows absolute amount of liquid resources
- Positive working capital = financial stability
- Negative working capital = potential cash flow problems

**Example:**
- Current Assets: €50,000
- Current Liabilities: €30,000
- Current Ratio: €50,000 ÷ €30,000 = 1.67 (good liquidity)
- Working Capital: €50,000 - €30,000 = €20,000 (positive, good)

**Both measures together provide:**
- Ratio shows relative strength
- Working capital shows absolute amount
- Together give complete liquidity picture

### Question 6: Current Ratio and Working Capital Calculation

**Answer:**

**Given:**
- Current Assets: €30,000
- Current Liabilities: €20,000

**Calculations:**

**Current Ratio:**
- Current Ratio = Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
- Current Ratio = €30,000 ÷ €20,000
- **Current Ratio = 1.5**

**Working Capital:**
- Working Capital = Current Assets - Current Liabilities
- Working Capital = €30,000 - €20,000
- **Working Capital = €10,000**

**Interpretation:**

**Current Ratio of 1.5:**
- Indicates adequate liquidity
- Business has €1.50 in current assets for every €1.00 in current liabilities
- Generally considered acceptable (above 1.0 threshold)
- Suggests ability to meet short-term obligations

**Working Capital of €10,000:**
- Positive working capital indicates financial stability
- Business has €10,000 more current assets than current liabilities
- Provides financial cushion for operations
- Suggests ability to handle unexpected expenses

**Overall Assessment:**
- **Liquidity Status:** Good
- **Financial Position:** Stable
- **Risk Level:** Low to moderate
- **Recommendation:** Monitor regularly, but current position is healthy

**Note:** While these numbers are acceptable, businesses should aim for:
- Current ratio > 1.5 for better safety margin
- Positive and growing working capital

### Question 7: Complete Accounting Cycle

**Answer:**

The complete accounting cycle consists of eight steps:

**Step 1: Identify and Record Transactions**
- Identify business events that affect the accounting equation
- Collect source documents (invoices, receipts, bank statements)
- Record transactions in journal (journal entries)
- Include PCN account numbers

**Step 2: Post to Ledger Accounts**
- Transfer journal entries to individual ledger accounts (T-accounts)
- Update account balances
- Organize transactions by account

**Step 3: Prepare Unadjusted Trial Balance**
- List all accounts and their balances
- Verify debits equal credits
- Check for posting errors
- Prepared before adjustments

**Step 4: Make Adjusting Entries**
- Record adjusting entries for:
  - Prepaid expenses
  - Unearned revenues
  - Accrued expenses
  - Accrued revenues
  - Depreciation
- Update accounts for accrual basis

**Step 5: Prepare Adjusted Trial Balance**
- List all accounts after adjustments
- Verify debits still equal credits
- Provides balances for financial statements

**Step 6: Prepare Financial Statements**
- Income Statement (from revenue and expense accounts)
- Statement of Owner's Equity (from equity accounts and net income)
- Balance Sheet (from asset, liability, and equity accounts)
- Statement of Cash Flows (if required)

**Step 7: Prepare Closing Entries**
- Close revenue accounts to Income Summary
- Close expense accounts to Income Summary
- Close Income Summary to Retained Earnings
- Close withdrawals (if applicable)

**Step 8: Prepare Post-Closing Trial Balance**
- List only permanent accounts
- Verify temporary accounts are zero
- Verify debits equal credits
- Shows starting balances for next period

**Cycle Complete!** Ready to begin next period with Step 1.

**Key Points:**
- Cycle repeats each accounting period
- Each step builds on previous steps
- All steps are necessary for accurate reporting
- Cycle ensures compliance with accounting principles

### Question 8: Luxembourg Annual Closing and RCS Filing Requirements

**Answer:**

**Annual Closing Requirements:**

1. **Complete Accounting Cycle:**
   - Record all transactions for the year
   - Make all adjusting entries
   - Prepare financial statements
   - Prepare closing entries
   - Prepare post-closing trial balance

2. **Financial Statements:**
   - Income Statement (Compte de Résultat)
   - Balance Sheet (Bilan)
   - Statement of Owner's Equity (if applicable)
   - Notes to Financial Statements (Annexe)

3. **PCN Compliance:**
   - All accounts must use PCN classifications
   - Financial statements must follow PCN format
   - Account numbers must be correct

**RCS Filing Requirements:**

1. **Filing Deadline:**
   - Must file within **7 months** of fiscal year end
   - Example: December 31 year end → file by July 31

2. **What Must Be Filed:**
   - Annual accounts (comptes annuels)
   - Income Statement
   - Balance Sheet
   - Notes to Financial Statements
   - Management report (for larger companies)

3. **Filing Format:**
   - Must be in PCN format
   - Must be in French, German, or English
   - Balance sheet and P&L prepared on eCDF, then filed electronically with the RCS via the LBR portal
   - Must be signed by company representatives

4. **Additional Requirements:**
   - Audit may be required (depending on size)
   - Must be accurate and complete
   - Must comply with Commercial Code

**Consequences of Non-Compliance:**
- Late filing penalties: €250-€25,000, depending on the violation
- RCS may reject incomplete filings
- Potential legal consequences
- Business reputation damage

### Question 9: Abbreviated vs. Full Financial Statements

**Answer:**

**Abbreviated Financial Statements (Comptes Abrégés):**

**When Allowed:**
Small companies, i.e. those not exceeding 2 of these 3 limits for two consecutive years:
- Net turnover ≤ €15 million
- Balance sheet total ≤ €7.5 million
- Average employees ≤ 50

**Characteristics:**
- Less detailed than full statements
- Fewer line items
- Simplified format
- Still must follow PCN structure

**Full Financial Statements (Comptes Complets):**

**When Required:**
- When 2 of the 3 limits are exceeded for two consecutive years:
  - Net turnover > €15 million
  - Balance sheet total > €7.5 million
  - Average employees > 50
- (Medium-sized companies may still abridge the P&L)

**Characteristics:**
- More detailed disclosures
- More line items
- Comprehensive format
- Additional information required

**Key Differences:**

| Aspect | Abbreviated | Full |
|--------|------------|------|
| **Detail Level** | Simplified | Comprehensive |
| **Disclosures** | Minimal | Extensive |
| **Line Items** | Fewer | More |
| **Notes** | Basic | Detailed |
| **Audit** | Not required (unless voluntary; an SA still needs a commissaire) | Required when 2 of 3 limits are exceeded for two consecutive years |

**Example:**
- Small SARL with €500,000 turnover, €500,000 assets, 5 employees → Abbreviated
- Larger SARL with €20 million turnover and €10 million balance sheet total (two consecutive years) → Full statements

**Both Must:**
- Follow PCN format
- Be filed with RCS
- Be accurate and complete
- Comply with Luxembourg regulations

### Question 10: Importance of Completing Accounting Cycle

**Answer:**

**Why It's Important:**

1. **Accurate Financial Reporting:**
   - Ensures financial statements are accurate
   - Reflects true financial position and performance
   - Required for stakeholders (investors, creditors, tax authorities)

2. **Compliance:**
   - Luxembourg requires complete accounting cycle
   - RCS filing requires properly closed accounts
   - PCN compliance requires proper procedures
   - Tax reporting requires accurate period-end balances

3. **Business Decision-Making:**
   - Accurate financial statements enable informed decisions
   - Period comparisons are meaningful
   - Budgeting and forecasting are reliable
   - Performance evaluation is accurate

4. **Period Separation:**
   - Each period's results are clearly separated
   - Prevents mixing of periods' activities
   - Enables accurate period comparisons

5. **Audit Readiness:**
   - Properly closed books are audit-ready
   - Reduces audit issues
   - Demonstrates good internal controls

**Consequences of Incomplete Closing:**

1. **Inaccurate Financial Statements:**
   - Revenues and expenses from multiple periods mixed
   - Cannot determine period performance
   - Misleading financial information

2. **Compliance Issues:**
   - RCS may reject annual accounts
   - Tax calculations may be incorrect
   - Potential penalties and fines

3. **Business Problems:**
   - Cannot make informed decisions
   - Cannot compare periods accurately
   - Cash flow management is difficult

4. **Operational Issues:**
   - Accounts accumulate incorrect balances
   - Next period starts with errors
   - Time-consuming to correct later

5. **Legal Consequences:**
   - Violation of Commercial Code
   - Potential criminal charges
   - Business reputation damage

**Best Practice:** Complete the accounting cycle monthly or at minimum quarterly to avoid problems.

### Problem A-5: Luxembourg Filing Deadlines

**Solution:**

**For a Business with December 31 Fiscal Year End:**

**Annual Accounts Filing (RCS):**
- **Deadline:** July 31 (7 months after year end)
- **What:** Annual accounts (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Notes)
- **Where:** RCS (Registre de Commerce et des Sociétés)
- **Format:** PCN format, electronic filing
- **Penalty for Late Filing:** €250-€25,000, depending on the violation

**Tax Return Filing (ACD):**
- **Deadline:** December 31 of the following year (corporate income tax and municipal business tax return, modèle 500)
- **What:** Corporate tax return (CIT and MBT declared together)
- **Where:** ACD (Administration des Contributions Directes)
- **Format:** Electronic filing via MyGuichet.lu
- **Penalty for Late Filing:** Late-filing surcharges and interest

**VAT Return Filing (if annual filer):**
- **Deadline:** Before March 1 of the following year (annual-only filers, turnover up to €112,000)
- **What:** Annual VAT return
- **Where:** AED (Administration de l'enregistrement, des domaines et de la TVA) through eCDF
- **Format:** Electronic filing
- **Penalty for Late Filing:** Fines and interest

**Note:** Businesses with turnover between €112,000 and €620,000 file quarterly returns and those above €620,000 file monthly returns (by the 15th of the following month); both also file an annual return before May 1.

**Summary Timeline (December 31 Year End):**
- Before March 1: Annual VAT return (annual-only filers)
- July 31: Annual accounts with RCS (approval within 6 months, filing within 1 month after)
- December 31 of the following year: Corporate tax return (modèle 500)

- **Required Action:** Must close accounts before filing

- **Problem:** Tax calculations based on incorrect financial statements
- **Impact:** May overpay or underpay taxes, potential penalties
- **Required Action:** Must have accurate period-end balances

   - Verify all accounts

   - Verify closing completed
   - Confirm temporary accounts are zero

   - Prepare correct annual financial statements
   - File with RCS if deadline hasn't passed

- May need to close each period separately
- Time-consuming but necessary
