# Solutions Manual - Chapter 14: Corporation Accounting

## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: Equity vs. Debt Financing
- **Equity financing** raises capital by issuing ownership interests (shares). No mandatory repayment; dividends are discretionary. Increases share capital (PCN 101) and possibly share premium (111).
- **Debt financing** raises funds through loans/bonds with contractual repayment and interest (PCN group 19: 1941/1942 bank loans, 1931/1932 bonds). Debt increases leverage and requires scheduled payments.

### Question 2: Share Issuance Above Par
- Record par portion in `101 Capital Social` (par value × number of shares).
- Excess proceeds go to `111 Prime d'émission` (share premium).
- Entry example: Debit Cash, Credit 101 (par), Credit 111 (premium).

### Question 3: Cash vs. Stock Dividends
- **Cash dividends** reduce retained earnings and cash; recognized when declared (Debit 141 Results brought forward, Credit 4712 Dividends Payable; payment debits 4712).
- **Stock dividends** reclassify equity: transfer from retained earnings to share capital/share premium, issue additional shares, no cash outflow.

### Question 4: EPS Calculation
- EPS = (Net Income − Preferred Dividends) ÷ Weighted Average Common Shares.
- Useful for comparing profitability per share, assessing dilution, and informing investors.

### Question 5: Luxembourg Corporate Forms
- **SARL:** min capital €12,000, limited to ≤100 shareholders, common for SMEs.
- **SA:** min capital €30,000, suitable for public markets, stricter governance.
- **SCS/SNC:** partnerships with unlimited liability for general partners. Accounting differs (owner’s equity vs. share capital) and compliance (RCS filings, statutory audits) varies.

## Problems Set A - Solutions

### Problem A-1: Share Issuance
1. Issue 1,000 shares @ €10 (par €1):
   - Debit 5131 Cash €10,000
   - Credit 101 Share Capital €1,000
   - Credit 111 Share Premium €9,000
2. Issue 500 shares @ €15:
   - Debit 5131 €7,500
   - Credit 101 €500
   - Credit 111 €7,000

### Problem A-2: Cash Dividend
1. Declaration:
   - Debit 141 Retained Earnings €5,000
   - Credit 4712 Dividends Payable €5,000
2. Payment:
   - Debit 4712 €5,000
   - Credit 5131 Cash €5,000
3. Retained earnings decreases by €5,000.

### Problem A-3: EPS
EPS = €50,000 ÷ 10,000 = **€5.00 per share**.

### Problem A-4: Stock Dividend (10%)
- Shares issued = 10% × 10,000 = 1,000 shares.
- Value transferred (small dividend, use market value) = 1,000 × €10 = €10,000.
- Entry:
  - Debit 141 Retained Earnings €10,000
  - Credit 101 Share Capital €1,000 (par)
  - Credit 111 Share Premium €9,000

### Problem A-5: Share Repurchase (Treasury Stock)
- Debit 502 Own Shares (Actions propres) €2,400
- Credit 5131 Cash €2,400
(Under Lux GAAP own shares are shown as an asset, 502, and an equivalent non-distributable reserve is required.)

## Comprehensive Problem 14 - Solutions

### 1. Share Issuances

  - Debit 5131 Cash €200,000

  - Credit 111 Share Premium €100,000

  - Debit 5131 Cash €50,000

### 2. Dividend Declarations/Payments
- 2024 cash dividend €30,000:
  - Declare: Debit 141 Retained Earnings €30,000 / Credit 4712 Dividends Payable €30,000
  - Pay: Debit 4712 Dividends Payable €30,000 / Credit 5131 Cash €30,000
- 2025 stock dividend 5% on 12,000 shares → 600 shares; assume market > par so use market (if not given, use par). Use given par €10 (conservative): transfer €6,000.
  - Debit 141 Retained Earnings €6,000
  - Credit 101 Share Capital €6,000 (if small stock dividend and market info absent, treat at par). If market value assumed €25, adjust accordingly (document assumption). Here adopt par to avoid overstating.
- 2025 cash dividend €40,000 same pattern.

### 3. Statement of Changes in Equity (summary)
**2024:**
- Share Capital: €100,000
- Share Premium: €100,000
- Retained Earnings: NI €80,000 − Dividend €30,000 = €50,000
- Total Equity 2024 end = €250,000

**2025:**
- Add new shares: Share Capital +€20,000 (issue) +€6,000 (stock dividend) = €126,000
- Share Premium +€30,000
- Retained Earnings: prior €50,000 + NI €100,000 − stock dividend €6,000 − cash dividend €40,000 = €104,000
- Total Equity 2025 end = €126,000 + €130,000 (premium cumulative) + €104,000 = €360,000

### 4. EPS
- 2024: EPS = 80,000 ÷ 10,000 = **€8.00**.
- 2025 weighted shares: start 10,000; +2,000 issued July 1 → weighted 1,000; stock dividend 5% treated as retroactive (×1.05). Weighted shares = (10,000 × 12/12 + 2,000 × 6/12) × 1.05 = (10,000 + 1,000) ×1.05 = 11,550. EPS 2025 = 100,000 ÷ 11,550 ≈ **€8.66**.

### 6. Luxembourg Requirements
- SARL minimum capital €12k fully subscribed; contributions recorded in PCN Class 1.
- Dividends allowed only if net assets remain ≥ subscribed capital + non-distributable reserves; approval by shareholders and filing in minutes. Before any distribution, 5% of the year's net profit must be allocated to the legal reserve (131) until it reaches 10% of subscribed capital (simplified away in this problem).
- PCN Class 1 accounts track capital (101), share premium (111), legal reserve (131), results brought forward (141) and result for the year (142); accurate classification needed for eCDF and RCS filings.
- RCS filings: incorporation deed, shareholder resolutions for dividends/capital changes, and annual accounts within one month of approval (at most 7 months after year-end).

## Case Solutions

### Case 14-1: Choosing Corporate Form
1. **Pros/cons:** SARL offers limited liability, simple governance; SA supports larger capital raising but more complex; sole proprietorship simplest but unlimited liability.
2. Capital: SARL ≥ €12k, SA ≥ €30k (25% paid-in at formation). Sole proprietorship no statutory minimum.
3. Accounting: Corporations use PCN Class 1 share-capital/reserve accounts; proprietorship uses owner’s equity in Class 1 but simpler disclosures.
4. Compliance: SARL/SA must file statutes, annual accounts, possible audit; sole proprietorship lighter but still tax filings.
5. Recommendation: SARL fits Marie’s SME scale, offers liability protection, easier to onboard partners.
6. Tax: Corporate income tax for SARL/SA (with potential dividend withholding); sole proprietorship taxed via personal income. Consider dividends vs. salary planning.

### Case 14-2: Dividend Policy
1. Decide based on cash needs, growth plans, legal reserves, and shareholder expectations.
2. Determine payout ratio; keep sufficient funds for CAPEX and working capital. Possibly adopt stable dividend + special bonuses.
3. Legal restrictions: protect minimum capital, ensure distributable reserves, respect Luxembourg Companies Law (Art. 197 et seq.).
4. Effects: Reduces cash and retained earnings; signals confidence but may limit reinvestment.
5. Tax: 15% dividend withholding (unless reduced by treaty/parent-subsidiary directive); shareholders taxed on dividends; company cannot deduct dividends.
6. Communication: Board proposal, shareholder approval, clear policy note in financial statements and investor communications.
