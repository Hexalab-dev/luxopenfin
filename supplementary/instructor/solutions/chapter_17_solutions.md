# Solutions Manual - Chapter 17: Financial Statement Analysis

## Questions - Solutions

1. **Why analysis is necessary:** Financial statement analysis turns raw figures into information about profitability, liquidity, solvency and efficiency. Owners, banks and investors use it to evaluate performance, spot trends and problems early, monitor loan covenants, and benchmark against Luxembourg SME norms.
2. **Comparative statements:** Placing two or more periods side by side with € and % changes (horizontal analysis) shows growth, trends and unusual movements (e.g., expenses growing faster than revenue) that a single period cannot reveal.
3. **Common-size statements:** Expressing each line as a % of revenue (income statement) or of total assets (balance sheet) removes the effect of size, so structure can be compared across years and with businesses of different sizes or industry benchmarks.
4. **Types of ratios:** Liquidity (current ratio, quick ratio), solvency (debt-to-equity, debt-to-assets, interest coverage), profitability (gross margin, net margin, ROA, ROE) and efficiency (inventory, receivables and asset turnover). Use consistent PCN classifications when computing them.
5. **ROA:** Net income ÷ average total assets. It measures how much profit each euro of assets generates; it can be split (DuPont) into profit margin × asset turnover to see whether returns come from pricing/cost control or from asset utilisation. Compare with prior years, the cost of debt and industry benchmarks.

## Problems Set A - Solutions

### Problem A-1: Comparative Income Statement
| | 2023 | 2024 | € change | % change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 200,000 | 240,000 | +40,000 | +20.0% |
| Expenses | 150,000 | 170,000 | +20,000 | +13.3% |
| Net income | 50,000 | 70,000 | +20,000 | +40.0% |

Expenses grew more slowly than revenue, so net income grew twice as fast as revenue.

### Problem A-2: Common-Size Income Statement
- Revenue €300,000 = 100%
- COGS €180,000 = 60.0%
- Gross profit €120,000 = 40.0%
- Operating expenses €80,000 = 26.7%
- Net income €40,000 = 13.3%

### Problem A-3: Liquidity Ratios
- Current Ratio = 120,000 / 60,000 = **2.00**
- Quick Ratio = (120,000 − 40,000) / 60,000 = **1.33**
- Interpretation: comfortable liquidity; even without inventory, quick assets cover current liabilities 1.33 times.

### Problem A-4: Profitability Ratios
- Gross Margin = 200,000 / 500,000 = **40%**
- Net Margin = 60,000 / 500,000 = **12%**
- ROA = 60,000 / 400,000 = **15%**
- ROE = 60,000 / 250,000 = **24%**

### Problem A-5: Efficiency Ratios
- Inventory Turnover = 240,000 / 60,000 = **4.0×** (≈ 91 days)
- Receivables Turnover = 400,000 / 50,000 = **8.0×** (≈ 46 days)
- Asset Turnover = 400,000 / 500,000 = **0.80×**

## Problems Set B - Solutions

### Problem B-1: Ratio Set
Total assets = 150,000 + 300,000 = €450,000; total liabilities = 75,000 + 150,000 = €225,000.
- Gross Margin = (600 − 360) / 600 = **40%**
- Operating margin = (600 − 360 − 180) / 600 = 10%; Net Margin = 60 / 600 = **10%**
- Current Ratio = 150 / 75 = **2.00**
- Quick Ratio = (150 − 50) / 75 = **1.33**
- Debt-to-Equity = 225 / 225 = **1.00** (long-term debt only: 150 / 225 = 0.67)
- Debt-to-Assets = 225 / 450 = **0.50**
- ROA = 60 / 450 = **13.3%** (year-end assets); ROE = 60 / 225 = **26.7%**
- Inventory Turnover = 360 / 45 = **8.0×**
- Receivables Turnover = 600 / 40 = **15.0×**
- Asset Turnover = 600 / 450 = **1.33×**

### Problem B-2: Comparative Statements
- Prepare side-by-side balance sheet and income statement showing 2023 vs. 2024 with columns for € change and % change (e.g., Cash +€10k, +25%).

### Problem B-3: Common-Size Statements
- Balance sheet: Express each asset/liability/equity item as % of total assets (e.g., Cash 45/300 = 15%).
- Income statement: Express line items as % of revenue.
- Analysis: highlight structural shifts (e.g., rising inventory share, shrinking margins).

### Problem B-4: DuPont Analysis
- Profit Margin = 80 / 500 = 16%
- Asset Turnover = 500 / 400 = 1.25×
- ROA = 0.16 × 1.25 = **20%**
- Interpretation: Evaluate whether focus should be on improving margins or turnover to enhance ROA.

## Comprehensive Problem 17 - Solutions

### 1. Comparative Income Statement
- Revenue +20%; COGS +20%; Operating expenses +16.7%; Net income +30%.
- Include table with absolute and percentage changes; note that operating leverage improved due to slower growth in operating expenses vs. revenue.

### 2. Comparative Balance Sheet
- Current assets +€20k (+20%); fixed assets +€50k (+25%).
- Current liabilities +€10k (+20%); long-term debt +€20k (+20%); equity +€40k (+26.7%).
- Trend: balance sheet expanded to support growth; leverage remains moderate.

### 3. Common-Size Income Statement
- 2023: COGS 60%, OpEx 30%, Net margin 10%.
- 2024: COGS 60%, OpEx 29.2%, Net margin 10.8% (slight improvement).

### 4. Common-Size Balance Sheet
- 2023: Current assets 33%, Fixed assets 67%, Liabilities 50%, Equity 50%.
- 2024: Current assets 32.4%, Fixed assets 67.6%, Liabilities 48.6%, Equity 51.4%.

### 5. Ratios
**Liquidity**
- Current Ratio 2023 = 100/50 = 2.0; 2024 = 120/60 = 2.0 (stable).
- Quick Ratio 2023 = (100−30)/50 = 1.4; 2024 = (120−40)/60 = 1.33 (slight decline).

**Solvency**
- Debt-to-Equity 2023 = 150/150 = 1.0; 2024 = 180/190 = 0.95 (improving).
- Debt-to-Assets 2023 = 150/300 = 0.50; 2024 = 180/370 ≈ 0.49.

**Profitability**
- Gross Margin constant at 40%.
- Net Margin: 10% (2023) → 10.8% (2024).
- ROA (year-end assets, no averages given): 40/300 = 13.3%; 52/370 = 14.1%.
- ROE (year-end equity): 40/150 = 26.7%; 52/190 = 27.4%.

**Efficiency**
- Inventory Turnover (given average inventories) 2023 = 240 / 28 ≈ 8.57×; 2024 = 288 / 35 ≈ 8.23× (slower).
- Receivables Turnover 2023 = 400 / 35 = 11.4×; 2024 = 480 / 42 = 11.4× (stable).
- Asset Turnover 2023 = 400 / 300 = 1.33×; 2024 = 480 / 370 = 1.30×.

### 6. Trend Analysis
- Improvements: profit margins, ROA/ROE slightly higher, leverage slightly lower.
- Concern: inventory turnover declining (build-up risk) and quick ratio trending down.
- Implications: need tighter inventory management and working-capital monitoring.

### 7. Luxembourg Benchmarks
- Compare ratios with Chamber of Commerce/STATEC SME averages (e.g., Quick ratio >1, Net margin 8–12%). Bistro is within healthy band but should maintain buffer.
- Strengths: profitability and leverage metrics above SME averages.
- Improvements: working-capital efficiency, cash buffer.

### 8. Recommendations
- Optimize inventory ordering to boost turnover and free cash.
- Monitor receivables aging even if turnover stable; consider early-payment incentives.
- Use additional profits to fund CAPEX without raising leverage further.
- Continue benchmarking with industry associations; update dashboards quarterly.

## Case Solutions

### Case 17-1: Performance Evaluation
1. Calculate liquidity, profitability, solvency, and efficiency ratios (current, quick, gross/net margin, ROA/ROE, turnover, coverage).
2. Compare results year-over-year and against Luxembourg restaurant benchmarks (STATEC, Horesca).
3. Relevant benchmarks: seat turnover, average check, food cost %, labor cost %, EBITDA margin.
4. KPIs: RevPAR (revenue per available seat), food cost %, labor cost %, cash conversion cycle.
5. Identify gaps (e.g., rising inventory days) and root causes; prioritize operational improvements.
6. Actions: adjust menu pricing, renegotiate supplier terms, improve staff scheduling, implement cash-flow forecasts.

### Case 17-2: Benchmarking
1. Use STATEC, Chamber of Commerce reports, and sector associations for benchmarks.
2. Focus on ratios tied to the business model (e.g., for services: utilization, EBITDA margin; for retail: inventory turnover).
3. Factor in size, geographic, and product differences; adjust for VAT regimes if cross-border.
4. Limitations: accounting policies differ, data lag, unique strategies; treat benchmarks as guidance, not absolutes.
5. Benchmarking informs target setting, budgeting, and early-warning dashboards.
6. Luxembourg specifics: align with PCN classifications, eCDF disclosures, and sector-specific grants/ KPIs.
