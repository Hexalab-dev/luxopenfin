# Solutions Manual - Chapter 13: Long-Term Liabilities

## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: Face Value vs. Present Value
**Answer:**
- **Face value** (par value) is the contractual amount to be repaid at maturity (e.g., €100,000 bond principal).
- **Present value** is the discounted value of future cash flows (principal + interest) using the market rate at issuance. Present value equals the amount recorded on the balance sheet at initial recognition (adjusted for premiums/discounts).

### Question 2: Effective-Interest Method
**Answer:**
- Interest expense = Carrying amount × market/effective rate each period.
- The difference between expense and cash paid adjusts the carrying amount (amortizes discounts/premiums).
- Preferred because it aligns expense recognition with economic cost and is required by IFRS/Lux GAAP for amortized-cost liabilities.

### Question 3: Bond Journal Entries
1. **Issuance at par:** Debit 5131 Cash / Credit 1932 Non-convertible bonds (> 1 year) for face amount.
2. **Interest payment:** Debit 6552 Interest Expense (or another 655 interest sub-account) / Credit 5131 Cash for coupon amount (plus amortization entries if discount/premium).
3. **Maturity:** Debit 1931 (reclassified from 1932 when due within one year) / Credit 5131 for face repayment.

### Question 4: PCN Accounts
- Group 19 (financial debts): 1931/1932 non-convertible bonds; 1941/1942 bank loans (residual maturity ≤ 1 year / > 1 year).
- The current portion is not a separate account: the instalment due within 12 months is moved from 1942 to 1941 at year-end.
- Lease liabilities and other loans: 47162/47262.
- Interest expense 6552; interest payable 4718 (short-term).

### Question 5: Importance of Proper Loan Accounting
Ensures accurate interest expense, covenant compliance, proper classification of current portion, correct disclosures (maturity schedule, interest rates), and alignment with tax deductions (interest is generally deductible in Luxembourg subject to interest limitation rules).

### Problem B-2: Loan Comparison
Use annuity formula Payment = P × [r / (1 − (1 + r)^-n)].
- Option A: r=5%, n=5 → Payment = 100,000 × [0.05 / (1−(1.05)^-5)] ≈ €23,097.48. Total paid ≈ €115,487 → interest ≈ €15,487.
- Option B: r=6%, n=7 → Payment ≈ €17,913.50; total ≈ €125,395 → interest ≈ €25,395.
Recommendation: Option A has lower total interest (≈ €9,900 less) but a higher annual payment (≈ €5,184 more). Choose A if cash flow allows; B if annual cash flow is the binding constraint.

### Problem B-3: Bond Accounting (€1,000,000, 5%, semi-annual)
- Issuance: Debit 5131 €1,000,000 / Credit 1932 €1,000,000.
- First interest (every six months): Interest = €1,000,000 × 5% × 6/12 = €25,000 → Debit 6552 €25,000 / Credit 5131 €25,000.
- Same entry each period, including the final payment at maturity. Repayment: Debit 1931 (reclassified from 1932 in the final year) €1,000,000 / Credit 5131 €1,000,000.

### Problem B-4: Finance Lease (PV €50,000, 6%, payment €12,000)
- At inception: Debit the tangible asset account (e.g., 222 or 2232) €50,000 / Credit 47262 Lease Liability (> 1 year) €50,000 (Lux GAAP has no 'right-of-use' asset; the leased asset is shown under tangible fixed assets).
- First payment interest = 50,000 × 6% = €3,000; principal = 12,000 − 3,000 = €9,000.
  - Debit 6552 Interest Expense €3,000
  - Debit 47262 Lease Liability €9,000
  - Credit 5131 Cash €12,000
  - Record depreciation separately (e.g., €50,000 ÷ lease term).

- Total interest over loan life = 38,851.37 × 10 − 300,000 = €88,513.70 (€88,513.73 in the schedule including the €0.03 final rounding).
- 2024 interest = 300,000 × 5% = €15,000.00.
- Principal reduction 2024 = 38,851.37 − 15,000.00 = €23,851.37.

### 7. Luxembourg Requirements
- **PCN Classification:** Bank loans recorded in Class 1, 1942 (> 1 year); the portion due within 12 months is moved to 1941.
- **Interest Tax Treatment:** Interest expense is deductible if arm’s length; monitor interest limitation rules (30% EBITDA / €3m safe harbor) per ACD guidance.
- **Documentation:** Maintain loan contracts, amortization schedules, bank confirmations for statutory audit.
- **Disclosures:** Notes to financial statements must show maturity schedule, interest rate, collateral, and covenant details.

## Case Solutions

### Case 13-1: Loan Decision
1. Annual payments (annuity formula as in Problem B-2):
   - Option A (5%, 10 years): Payment = 200,000 × [0.05 / (1 − (1.05)^-10)] ≈ €25,900.91.
   - Option B (6%, 7 years): Payment ≈ €35,827.00.
2. Total interest: Option A ≈ €25,900.91 × 10 − 200,000 = €59,009. Option B ≈ €35,827.00 × 7 − 200,000 = €50,789.
3. Option A cheaper annual payment but longer term / more total interest; Option B shorter term, higher payment, less total interest.
4. Recommendation depends on cash flow tolerance; if monthly cash tight, Option A. If goal is minimize interest and business can afford higher payment, Option B.
5. Accounting: record loan liability in 1942 (current instalment in 1941), amortize using effective-interest schedule, reclassify current portion yearly.
6. Cash flow: ensure liquidity for annual payments, budget for social charges, VAT, etc. Consider covenant impact.

### Case 13-2: Long-Term Debt Management
1. Improve debt management via refinancing, consolidating loans, extending maturities, or negotiating covenants.
2. Financial statements show interest expense (profit & loss) and split between current/non-current liabilities; high leverage impacts balance sheet strength.
3. Ratios affected: debt-to-equity, interest coverage, gearing, operating cash flow coverage.
4. Options: refinance with lower rate, seek government-backed loans (SNCI), sell assets, inject equity, adjust payment schedules.
5. Monitoring: maintain amortization schedules, covenant trackers, cash forecasting, scenario planning.
6. Luxembourg requirements: comply with PCN classification, provide disclosures in notes, ensure interest deductibility documentation, track hedging arrangements if any.
