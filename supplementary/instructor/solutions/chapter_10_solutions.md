## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: FIFO vs. Weighted Average

**Answer:**

**FIFO (First-In, First-Out):**
- Assumes oldest inventory is sold first
- Ending inventory consists of most recent purchases
- In rising prices: Lower COGS, higher ending inventory, higher net income
- In falling prices: Higher COGS, lower ending inventory, lower net income

**Weighted Average:**
- Calculates average cost of all inventory
- Uses average cost for both COGS and ending inventory
- Smoothes out price fluctuations
- More stable results

**When Each is Appropriate:**

**FIFO:**
- When prices are rising (shows higher profits)
- When inventory is perishable (matches physical flow)
- When want to show current inventory value
- Common in Luxembourg

**Weighted Average:**
- When prices fluctuate significantly
- When want to smooth out cost variations
- When inventory is homogeneous
- Also common in Luxembourg

**Key Difference:**
- FIFO: Uses specific costs (oldest first)
- Weighted Average: Uses average cost of all units

### Question 2: Periodic vs. Perpetual Systems

**Answer:**

**Periodic Inventory System:**
- Inventory and COGS determined only at period end
- Purchases recorded in Purchases account
- No continuous inventory tracking
- COGS calculated: Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory

**Perpetual Inventory System:**
- Inventory and COGS updated continuously
- Purchases recorded directly to Inventory
- Continuous inventory tracking
- COGS recorded at time of sale

**Cost of Goods Sold Calculation:**

**Periodic:**
- COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory
- Calculated at period end
- Requires physical count

**Perpetual:**
- COGS recorded at each sale
- Based on cost flow assumption (FIFO, weighted average)
- Continuous calculation

**Example:**

**Periodic:**
- Beginning: €10,000
- Purchases: €50,000
- Ending: €15,000
- COGS: €10,000 + €50,000 - €15,000 = €45,000

**Perpetual:**
- Each sale: Record COGS immediately
- Example: Sell 100 units @ €10 = €1,000 COGS
- Total COGS = Sum of all sales' costs

### Question 3: Inventory Error Impact

**Answer:**

**If Ending Inventory Overstated by €3,000:**

**Impact on Cost of Goods Sold:**
- COGS = Beginning + Purchases - Ending
- If Ending overstated by €3,000, COGS is understated by €3,000
- **COGS: Understated by €3,000**

**Impact on Gross Profit:**
- Gross Profit = Sales - COGS
- If COGS understated by €3,000, Gross Profit overstated by €3,000
- **Gross Profit: Overstated by €3,000**

**Impact on Net Income:**
- Net Income = Gross Profit - Operating Expenses
- If Gross Profit overstated by €3,000, Net Income overstated by €3,000
- **Net Income: Overstated by €3,000**

**Impact on Balance Sheet:**
- Assets: Inventory overstated by €3,000
- Equity: Retained Earnings overstated by €3,000 (through net income)
- **Assets and Equity: Both overstated by €3,000**

**Summary:**
- COGS: Understated €3,000
- Gross Profit: Overstated €3,000
- Net Income: Overstated €3,000
- Assets: Overstated €3,000
- Equity: Overstated €3,000

### Question 4: Inventory Turnover

**Answer:**

**Formula:**
- Inventory Turnover = Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Inventory
- Average Inventory = (Beginning Inventory + Ending Inventory) ÷ 2

**What It Indicates:**

**High Turnover:**
- Inventory sold quickly
- Good inventory management
- Less risk of obsolescence
- Lower carrying costs

**Low Turnover:**
- Inventory sold slowly
- Poor inventory management
- Higher risk of obsolescence
- Higher carrying costs

**Days Sales in Inventory:**
- Days Sales in Inventory = 365 ÷ Inventory Turnover
- Shows how many days inventory is held
- Lower is generally better

**Example:**
- COGS: €80,000
- Beginning: €12,000
- Ending: €18,000
- Average: (€12,000 + €18,000) ÷ 2 = €15,000
- Turnover: €80,000 ÷ €15,000 = 5.33 times
- Days: 365 ÷ 5.33 = 68.5 days

### Question 5: Self-Correcting Inventory Errors

**Answer:**

**Why Errors Are Self-Correcting:**

**Example:**
- Year 1: Ending inventory overstated by €3,000
  - Year 1 COGS understated by €3,000
  - Year 1 Net Income overstated by €3,000
  - Year 2 Beginning inventory overstated by €3,000
- Year 2: Ending inventory correct
  - Year 2 COGS overstated by €3,000 (because beginning was overstated)
  - Year 2 Net Income understated by €3,000
- **Total over 2 years: Net effect = €0** (self-corrected)

**Why This Is Still a Problem:**

1. **Period Accuracy:**
   - Each period's financial statements are incorrect
   - Cannot compare periods accurately
   - Misleading information

2. **Decision-Making:**
   - Decisions based on incorrect information
   - Wrong period shows better/worse performance
   - Poor business decisions

3. **Compliance:**
   - Financial statements must be accurate each period
   - Tax calculations incorrect
   - RCS filing incorrect

4. **Stakeholders:**
   - Investors, creditors misled
   - Wrong period performance
   - Loss of trust

5. **Cash Flow:**
   - Incorrect period cash flow analysis
   - Wrong planning
   - Poor cash management

**Best Practice:** Prevent errors through proper controls and procedures.

### Question 7: LIFO Under Luxembourg GAAP vs. IFRS

**Answer:**

**Is LIFO allowed?**

1. **Luxembourg GAAP:**
   - The law of 19 December 2002 (art. 66, valuation of fungible items) permits weighted average, FIFO, LIFO or a similar method for fungible inventory
   - The method chosen must be applied consistently and disclosed in the notes
   - The PCN chart of accounts does not prescribe a cost-flow method

2. **IFRS:**
   - IAS 2 prohibits LIFO; only FIFO and weighted average cost (plus specific identification for non-interchangeable items) are allowed
   - A Luxembourg company reporting under IFRS therefore cannot use LIFO

**Why most Luxembourg SMEs still use FIFO or weighted average:**

1. **Physical flow:** FIFO matches the actual flow of perishable goods (oldest used first)
2. **Balance sheet relevance:** with rising prices LIFO leaves old, low costs in ending inventory, so the balance sheet understates current value
3. **Comparability:** FIFO and weighted average are accepted under both Lux GAAP and IFRS, which helps groups, banks and investors compare figures

**Implications of choosing LIFO (rising prices):**
- Higher COGS, lower ending inventory, lower reported profit
- Since taxable profit generally follows accounting profit, profit (and tax) is deferred, not eliminated
- Must be applied consistently and disclosed; a later change of method needs justification and disclosure in the notes

**Allowed Methods (Luxembourg GAAP):**
- FIFO (First-In, First-Out)
- Weighted Average
- LIFO or a similar method (fungible items)
- Specific Identification (for unique items)

### Question 8: Weighted Average - Periodic vs. Perpetual

**Answer:**

**Periodic Weighted Average:**
- Average calculated once at period end
- Uses all purchases for the period
- Single average cost for all sales
- Simpler calculation

**Perpetual Weighted Average:**
- Average recalculated after each purchase
- Moving average cost
- Each sale uses current average
- More complex but more accurate

**Example:**

**Periodic:**
- Beginning: 100 units @ €10 = €1,000
- Purchase 1: 150 units @ €12 = €1,800
- Purchase 2: 200 units @ €14 = €2,800
- Total: 450 units, €5,600
- Average: €5,600 ÷ 450 = €12.44
- All sales use €12.44

**Perpetual:**
- After Purchase 1: (€1,000 + €1,800) ÷ 250 = €11.20
- Sale uses €11.20
- After Purchase 2: Recalculate average
- Next sale uses new average

**Why Different:**
- Periodic: One calculation, all sales same cost
- Perpetual: Continuous calculation, each sale uses current average

### Question 9: Choosing Inventory Valuation Method

**Answer:**

**Factors to Consider:**

1. **Price Trends:**
   - Rising prices: FIFO shows higher profits
   - Falling prices: FIFO shows lower profits
   - Fluctuating prices: Weighted average smooths

2. **Inventory Type:**
   - Perishable: FIFO matches physical flow
   - Homogeneous: Weighted average works well
   - Unique items: Specific identification

3. **Business Goals:**
   - Want higher profits: FIFO (in rising prices)
   - Want stability: Weighted average
   - Want accuracy: Specific identification

4. **Compliance:**
   - Must follow PCN requirements
   - Must be consistent
   - Must disclose method

5. **Tax Considerations:**
   - Tax impact of method
   - Consistency required
   - Cannot change without reason

6. **System Capability:**
   - Perpetual system: Can use either method
   - Periodic system: Either method works
   - System must support chosen method

**Recommendation:**
- Consider all factors
- Choose method that best fits business
- Be consistent
- Document decision

### Problem A-1: FIFO Calculation (Periodic)

**Solution:**

**Given:**
- Beginning Inventory: 100 units @ €10 = €1,000
- Purchase 1: 150 units @ €12 = €1,800
- Purchase 2: 200 units @ €14 = €2,800
- Ending Inventory: 180 units

**Total Available:**
- Units: 100 + 150 + 200 = 450 units
- Cost: €1,000 + €1,800 + €2,800 = €5,600

**Units Sold:**
- 450 - 180 = 270 units

**FIFO Calculation:**

**Ending Inventory (180 units - most recent):**
- 180 units from Purchase 2 @ €14 = €2,520

**Cost of Goods Sold:**
- Beginning: 100 units @ €10 = €1,000
- Purchase 1: 150 units @ €12 = €1,800
- Purchase 2: 20 units @ €14 = €280
- **Total COGS: €3,080**

**Verification:**
- COGS: €3,080
- Ending Inventory: €2,520
- Total: €5,600 ✓

### Problem A-2: Weighted Average (Periodic)

**Solution:**

**Using Same Data:**

**Total Available:**
- Units: 450
- Cost: €5,600
- **Average Cost: €5,600 ÷ 450 = €12.444 per unit**

**Ending Inventory:**
- 180 units × €12.444 = **€2,240** (€5,600 × 180/450)

**Cost of Goods Sold:**
- 270 units × €12.444 = **€3,360** (€5,600 × 270/450)

**Verification:**
- COGS: €3,360
- Ending Inventory: €2,240
- Total: €5,600 ✓

**Comparison:**
- FIFO Ending Inventory: €2,520
- Weighted Average Ending Inventory: €2,240
- FIFO shows higher ending inventory (prices rising)

### Comprehensive Problem 10: Complete Inventory Management

**Solution:**

#### 1. FIFO Method (Periodic)

**Given:**
- Beginning: 500 units @ €20 = €10,000
- Feb 15: 800 units @ €22 = €17,600
- May 10: 1,200 units @ €24 = €28,800
- Aug 20: 1,000 units @ €26 = €26,000

- Units: 500 + 800 + 1,200 + 1,000 + 600 = 4,100 units
- Cost: €10,000 + €17,600 + €28,800 + €26,000 + €16,800 = €99,200

**Units Sold: 3,000 units**

**COGS (oldest first):**
- Beginning: 500 units @ €20 = €10,000
- Feb 15: 800 units @ €22 = €17,600
- May 10: 1,200 units @ €24 = €28,800
- Aug 20: 500 units @ €26 = €13,000

**b) Ending Inventory (FIFO):**

**Ending Inventory (1,100 units - most recent):**
- Aug 20: 500 units @ €26 = €13,000
- Nov 5: 600 units @ €28 = €16,800
- **Total Ending Inventory: €29,800**

**c) Verification:**
- COGS: €69,400
- Ending: €29,800
- Total: €99,200 ✓

#### 2. Weighted Average Method (Periodic)

**a) Average Cost Per Unit:**
- Total Cost: €99,200
- Total Units: 4,100
- **Average: €99,200 ÷ 4,100 = €24.1951 per unit**

**b) Cost of Goods Sold:**
- 3,000 units × €24.1951 = **€72,585.37**

**c) Ending Inventory:**
- 1,100 units × €24.1951 = **€26,614.63**
- Check: €72,585.37 + €26,614.63 = €99,200 ✓

**d) Comparison to FIFO:**
- FIFO COGS: €69,400
- Weighted Average COGS: €72,585.37
- Difference: €3,185.37 (Weighted Average higher)

- FIFO Ending: €29,800
- Weighted Average Ending: €26,614.63
- Difference: €3,185.37 (FIFO higher)

**Why Different:**
- Prices rising throughout year
- FIFO uses older (lower) costs for COGS
- Weighted Average uses average (higher) costs
- FIFO shows higher ending inventory value

   - Faster sales
   - Better inventory mix
   - Reduce slow-moving items
   - Clearance sales

3. **Target:**
   - Aim for 4.5-5 times turnover
   - Reduce to 70-80 days
   - Match or exceed industry

4. **Monitoring:**
   - Track turnover monthly
   - Monitor aging
   - Review regularly
   - Take action

#### 5. Error Analysis

**If Ending Inventory Counted as 1,200 units (100 units too high):**

**Correct Ending (1,100 units, FIFO):**
- Ending: €29,800 (from FIFO)

**Incorrect Ending (1,200 units):**
- Additional 100 units @ €28 (latest cost under FIFO) = €2,800
- Incorrect Ending: €29,800 + €2,800 = €32,600

**a) Impact on Cost of Goods Sold:**
- Correct COGS: €69,400
- Incorrect COGS: €69,400 - €2,800 = €66,600
- **COGS: Understated by €2,800**

**b) Impact on Gross Profit:**
- If COGS understated by €2,800, Gross Profit overstated by €2,800
- **Gross Profit: Overstated by €2,800**

**c) Impact on Net Income:**
- If Gross Profit overstated by €2,800, Net Income overstated by €2,800
- **Net Income: Overstated by €2,800**

**d) Impact on Balance Sheet:**
- Inventory: Overstated by €2,800
- Assets: Overstated by €2,800
- Equity: Overstated by €2,800 (through net income)

### Case 10-1: Choosing an Inventory Method

**Solution:**

#### 1. Most Appropriate Method by Inventory Type

| Inventory | Characteristics | Recommended method | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Food ingredients | Perishable, prices rising | **FIFO** | Matches physical flow (oldest used first); ending stock at recent prices |
| Wine | Non-perishable, prices stable | **Weighted average** (or FIFO) | With stable prices both give similar results; average is simple to maintain |
| Retail merchandise | Various items, varying prices | **Weighted average** | Smooths price fluctuations across many items |

#### 2. Different Methods for Different Types?

Yes. A method may be chosen per category of similar items, provided it is applied consistently from year to year and disclosed in the notes. The same method must be used for all items of the same nature. LIFO is also permitted under Luxembourg GAAP for fungible items, but it would understate wine and food stock in a period of rising prices and is rarely used by SMEs.

#### 3. Tax Implications in Luxembourg

- Taxable profit generally follows the accounting profit, so the method affects CIT/MBT timing
- With rising food prices, weighted average gives higher COGS and slightly lower taxable profit than FIFO; FIFO gives higher profit and tax in the short term
- The difference is a timing difference that reverses over time; changing method to reduce tax is not acceptable without valid justification

#### 4. Effect on Financial Statements

- **FIFO (rising prices):** lower COGS, higher gross profit, higher inventory on the balance sheet
- **Weighted average:** COGS and inventory between oldest and newest costs; smoother margins
- With stable wine prices, the effect is minimal

#### 5. Factors to Consider

- Physical flow and perishability; price trends
- Simplicity and the capability of her POS/accounting software
- Consistency and comparability between years
- Tax and cash-flow effects; advice from her accountant

#### 6. Disclosure Requirements in Luxembourg

- The notes to the annual accounts must state the valuation methods applied to inventories (cost-flow method, lower of cost and market value)
- Any change of method, the reasons and its effect must be disclosed
- Inventory is shown in the balance sheet under current assets (PCN class 3: 361 for food, drinks and goods for resale)

### Case 10-2: Inventory Management Problems

**Solution:**

#### 1. Current Inventory Turnover

- Turnover = 365 ÷ days sales in inventory = 365 ÷ 90 = **4.06 times per year**
- Not acceptable for a retailer suffering stockouts and high storage costs: stock is held for about three months, yet the right items are not available. Many retailers aim for shorter holding periods (e.g., 45–60 days, i.e., 6–8 turns), depending on the product range.

#### 2. Causes

- Poor demand forecasting and purchasing based on habit rather than sales data
- Wrong product mix: too much slow-moving stock, too little of fast sellers
- No reorder points or safety-stock rules per item
- Infrequent physical counts; records not reliable

#### 3. Improvement Strategies

- ABC analysis: focus control on the items with the highest value/sales
- Set reorder points and reorder quantities per item based on sales velocity
- Clear slow-moving items (markdowns, returns to suppliers) and stop reordering them
- Negotiate smaller, more frequent deliveries with suppliers

#### 4. Reducing Levels Without Stockouts

- Use perpetual inventory data from the POS to reorder fast sellers automatically
- Keep safety stock only for fast-moving items; cut stock of slow movers
- Review weekly sales reports and adjust orders

#### 5. Systems and Procedures

- Perpetual inventory system integrated with POS and accounting
- Regular cycle counts and annual full count; investigate variances
- Monthly aging/slow-moving report; approval rules for purchases

#### 6. Effect on Financial Performance

- Excess stock ties up cash and increases storage, insurance and financing costs
- Slow movers may need write-downs to market value (reducing profit)
- Stockouts lose sales and margin
- Better management improves cash flow, gross margin and return on assets
