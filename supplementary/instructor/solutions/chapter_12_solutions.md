# Solutions Manual - Chapter 12: Current Liabilities

## Questions - Solutions

### Question 1: Definition and Examples
**Answer:** Current liabilities are obligations due within one year or the normal operating cycle, whichever is longer. Typical examples include accounts payable (PCN 44111), VAT payable (461411 output VAT / 461412 payable to the AED), salaries payable (4714), social charges payable to CCSS (4621, employee and employer portions), wage tax withheld (46124), bank overdrafts (5132), current portion of long-term bank debt (1941), accrued expenses / invoices not yet received (44112, 4718), and unearned revenue (482).

### Question 2: VAT Payable Calculation and Recording
**Answer:** VAT payable equals output VAT collected on taxable sales minus recoverable input VAT on purchases. Journal entries:
- Sales: Debit 4011 Customers / Credit 7061 Sales (excluding VAT) / Credit 461411 VAT Payable (output VAT).
- Purchases: Debit expense or inventory / Debit 421611 VAT Recoverable (input VAT) / Credit 44111 Suppliers.
At period end, both are cleared: Debit 461411 / Credit 421611 / Credit 461412 VAT payable to the AED (or Debit 421612 VAT credit if input exceeds output), then Debit 461412 / Credit 5131 Bank upon payment. VAT returns are filed via eCDF — monthly (turnover > €620,000), quarterly (€112,000–€620,000) or annually (≤ €112,000), periodic returns by the 15th of the following month; separate tracking per rate (17%, 14%, 8%, 3%) is required.

### Question 3: Social Charges Accounting
**Answer:** Luxembourg employers withhold employee contributions (12.95%: health 2.80% + 0.25%, pension 8.50%, dependency 1.40%) and record employer contributions (12.57% with Mutualité class 1: health 2.80% + 0.25%, pension 8.50%, accident 0.65%, health at work 0.14%, Mutualité 0.23%) — CCSS rates as of 1 January 2026, check ccss.lu each year; dependency is strictly on gross minus 1/4 of the social minimum wage, simplified here to full gross. When payroll is recognized:
- Debit 6211 Salaries Expense (gross)
- Credit 4714 Salaries Payable (net pay)
- Credit 4621 CCSS Payable – employee portion (and 46124 for wage tax withheld)
- Employer portion: Debit 623 Employer Social Charges / Credit 4621 CCSS Payable.
Payments to CCSS settle both components (Debit 4621 / Credit 5131). Deadlines typically the 15th of the following month.

### Question 4: PCN Accounts for Current Liabilities
**Answer:** Key PCN Class 4 accounts include:
- 43: Payments received on account (4311 customer deposits)
- 44: Trade payables (44111 suppliers, 44112 invoices not yet received) and notes payable (4421)
- 46: Tax and social security debts (461411 output VAT, 461412 VAT payable to the AED, 46121/46122 CIT/MBT payable, 46124 wage tax withheld, 4621 CCSS)
- 47: Other debts (4714 salaries payable, 4712 dividends payable, 4718 sundry, e.g. interest payable)
- 48: Deferred income (482 unearned revenue)
- Class 1 also holds the current portion of bank loans (1941).

### Question 5: Importance of Accurate Current Liability Recording
**Answer:** Proper tracking ensures liquidity planning, compliance with VAT/CCSS deadlines, accurate financial statements, reliable PCN/eCDF filings, and avoidance of penalties. Misstatements affect working capital management, covenant calculations, and stakeholder trust.

### Problem A-2: VAT Payable
- Output VAT: €20,000 ×17% = €3,400; €5,000 ×3% = €150 → **€3,550**.
- Input VAT: €12,000 ×17% = €2,040; €3,000 ×3% = €90 → **€2,130**.
- Net VAT payable = €3,550 − €2,130 = **€1,420**.
- Entry to settle:
  - Debit 461411 VAT Payable €3,550
  - Credit 421611 VAT Recoverable €2,130
  - Credit 5131 Bank €1,420

### Problem B-2: VAT Return
- Output VAT: 17% × €50,000 = €8,500; 3% × €10,000 = €300 → total **€8,800**.
- Input VAT: 17% × €30,000 = €5,100; 3% × €5,000 = €150 → total **€5,250**.
- Net VAT payable = €3,550.
- Entry: Debit 461411 €8,800 / Credit 421611 €5,250 / Credit 5131 €3,550.

### Problem B-3: Payroll Cycle

- Employer contributions: 12.57% × €20,000 = €2,514.

   - Debit 6211 €20,000

   - Credit 4621 Social Charges Payable €2,590

2. Employer contribution:
   - Debit 623 €2,514 / Credit 4621 €2,514
3. Pay salaries:
   - Debit 4714 €15,410 / Credit 5131 €15,410

   - Debit 4621 €5,104 (€2,590 + €2,514) / Credit 5131 €5,104

   - Debit 46124 €2,000 / Credit 5131 €2,000 (paid to the ACD)

Monitor working capital to ensure cash availability for €43,500 obligations.

## Case Solutions

### Case 12-1: VAT Compliance Issues
1. Late filing consequences: penalties (up to 10%), interest, potential audits, suspension of VAT registration, and cash-flow strain.
2. Tracking VAT by rate: use accounting software or spreadsheets with separate ledgers for each VAT rate, integrate POS systems, reconcile monthly.
3. Steps for timely filing: set calendar reminders, delegate responsibility, close books promptly, reconcile VAT control accounts weekly.
4. Prevent future issues: implement internal controls, maintain documentation, perform periodic reconciliations, conduct staff training.
5. Professional help: engage fiduciaire, VAT consultant, or certified accountant.
6. Operational benefits: accurate pricing, cash forecasting, compliance reputation, easier eCDF filings.

### Case 12-2: Payroll and Social Charges Management
1. Social charges finance social security; rates are high, impacting labor costs and compliance risk.
2. Calculations: use payroll software to compute gross-to-net, employee vs. employer shares, and journal entries (6211 gross salaries and 623 employer charges; 4621 CCSS, 46124 wage tax and 4714 net pay liabilities).
3. Systems: dedicated payroll software, CCSS online services, checklists for onboarding, segregation of duties.
4. Deadlines: wages as per employment contracts; social charges due by 15th of next month; income tax withholding remitted monthly.
5. Compliance: maintain accurate employee records, update rates, archive payslips and CCSS declarations.
6. Cost implications: employer social charges add ~12.6–15% on top of gross payroll (12.57% with Mutualité class 1), while the employee's 12.95% is withheld from gross (CCSS rates as of 1 January 2026); plan budgets, consider government subsidies (ADEM, SNCI) when hiring.
