# Example Consistency Guidelines

This document provides guidelines for maintaining consistency in examples throughout the Luxembourg SME Accounting Textbook, ensuring effective learning through coherent, progressive examples.

## Purpose

Consistent examples help students:
- Build understanding progressively
- See how concepts connect across chapters
- Relate to familiar business scenarios
- Understand business evolution
- Apply concepts to realistic situations

## Primary vs. Secondary Examples

### Primary Examples (Recurring Characters)

**When to Use:**
- Most examples in the textbook
- When building on previous examples
- When showing concept progression
- When demonstrating business evolution
- When consistency aids learning

**Primary Character:** Marie Dubois / Le Petit Bistro
- Use for 60-70% of examples
- Follow business evolution timeline
- Maintain financial consistency
- Build on previous chapter examples

**Benefits:**
- Students become familiar with the business
- Examples build on each other
- Realistic business progression
- Easier to see concept connections

### Secondary Examples (Different Characters)

**When to Use:**
- Different industry perspectives
- Contrasting business types
- Specialized scenarios
- When primary character doesn't fit
- To provide variety

**Secondary Characters:**
- Boulangerie Schmidt (bakery, family business)
- TechStart Luxembourg (technology startup)
- Generic retail shop (when type doesn't matter)

**Benefits:**
- Shows concept applicability across industries
- Provides variety
- Demonstrates different scenarios
- Avoids over-reliance on one business

### Generic Examples

**When to Use:**
- Simple concept illustrations
- When business type doesn't matter
- Theoretical scenarios
- Basic demonstrations
- When specific details would distract

**Benefits:**
- Focus on concept, not business details
- Simple and clear
- Easy to understand
- No character consistency needed

## Maintaining Continuity

### Financial Consistency

**Rules:**
- Account balances must carry forward logically
- Financial statements must reflect all previous transactions
- Numbers must be realistic for business size
- Growth must be believable

**Example:**
- If Chapter 3 shows Cash balance of €15,000
- Chapter 4 must account for that balance
- Subsequent chapters must maintain consistency
- Year-end statements must reconcile

### Timeline Consistency

**Rules:**
- Follow the business evolution timeline
- Events must occur in logical order
- Business growth must be gradual
- No time travel or contradictions

**Timeline:**
- Chapters 1-5: Year 1 (startup)
- Chapters 6-10: Year 2 (growth)
- Chapters 11-17: Year 3 (maturity)

### Character Consistency

**Rules:**
- Use consistent names and spellings
- Maintain character details
- Keep business information consistent
- Don't contradict previous information

**Example:**
- Always use "Marie Dubois" (not variations)
- Le Petit Bistro is always a restaurant
- Location is always Luxembourg City
- Business type remains consistent

## Progressive Complexity

### Simple Examples (Introduction)

**Characteristics:**
- Single concept focus
- Minimal details
- Clear and straightforward
- Easy to understand

**When to Use:**
- Introducing new concepts
- First examples in a section
- Basic illustrations
- Foundation building

**Example:**
"Marie purchases ingredients for €100 cash. This transaction increases Inventory (asset) and decreases Cash (asset)."

### Intermediate Examples (Development)

**Characteristics:**
- Multiple concepts
- More realistic details
- Moderate complexity
- Building on simple examples

**When to Use:**
- After simple examples
- Developing understanding
- Showing connections
- Progressive learning

**Example:**
"Marie purchases ingredients for €500, paying €200 cash and owing €300 to the supplier. This affects Inventory, Cash, and Accounts Payable accounts, and includes €85 VAT."

### Complex Examples (Application)

**Characteristics:**
- Multiple concepts integrated
- Real-world complexity
- Full business scenarios
- Comprehensive application

**When to Use:**
- End of sections
- Comprehensive problems
- Case studies
- Real-world application

**Example:**
"Marie's restaurant completes a catering event. The transaction involves advance payment, inventory usage, labor costs, VAT calculations, revenue recognition, and multiple account entries across the accounting cycle."

## Example Progression Within Chapters

### Section Introduction

**Start with Simple:**
- Basic concept illustration
- Single transaction
- Clear and straightforward
- Easy to follow

### Section Development

**Progress to Intermediate:**
- Multiple transactions
- More realistic scenarios
- Building complexity
- Connecting concepts

### Section Conclusion

**End with Complex:**
- Comprehensive examples
- Real-world scenarios
- Multiple concepts integrated
- Full application

## Introducing New Examples

### When to Introduce New Characters

**Appropriate Times:**
- Different industry needed
- Contrasting scenarios
- Specialized topics
- Variety needed
- Primary character doesn't fit

**Process:**
1. Check if primary character works
2. If not, select appropriate secondary character
3. Introduce clearly
4. Use consistently if recurring
5. Document in character database

### How to Introduce New Examples

**Introduction Format:**
- Provide business context
- Explain business type
- Give relevant details
- Connect to learning objective
- Make relatable

**Example:**
"Boulangerie Schmidt, a family-owned bakery in Esch-sur-Alzette, has been operating for 20 years. Unlike Marie's new restaurant, this established business has different inventory management challenges..."

## Example Quality Standards

### Effective Examples Should:

1. **Be Relevant:** Directly support learning objectives
2. **Be Realistic:** Reflect real business situations
3. **Be Clear:** Easy to understand and follow
4. **Be Progressive:** Build complexity gradually
5. **Be Consistent:** Maintain continuity across chapters
6. **Be Luxembourg-Specific:** Include local context
7. **Be Engaging:** Relatable and interesting

### Avoid:

1. **Contradictions:** Don't contradict previous examples
2. **Unrealistic Scenarios:** Keep examples believable
3. **Excessive Complexity:** Don't overwhelm students
4. **Inconsistency:** Maintain character/business details
5. **Irrelevance:** Only include relevant details
6. **Confusion:** Keep examples clear and focused

## Luxembourg-Specific Considerations

### Business Context

**Include:**
- Luxembourg business environment
- Local regulations and requirements
- PCN account references
- VAT considerations
- Local business practices

**Example:**
"Marie's restaurant must comply with Luxembourg's PCN requirements, using account 510000 for Cash and account 430000 for VAT Payable, and filing VAT returns through the eCDF system."

### Cultural Context

**Reflect:**
- Multilingual environment
- Luxembourg business culture
- Local business practices
- Regional considerations

**Example:**
"Marie's restaurant serves both local Luxembourg customers and international clients, requiring multilingual menus and understanding of diverse customer preferences."

## Cross-Chapter Example Connections

### Building on Previous Examples

**Format:**
- Reference previous examples
- Show how concepts connect
- Build on established scenarios
- Maintain continuity

**Example:**
"In Chapter 3, we recorded Marie's initial equipment purchase. Now, in Chapter 11, we examine how to account for depreciation of that equipment over time."

### Forward-Looking Examples

**Format:**
- Introduce concepts briefly
- Indicate where detailed later
- Prepare for future topics
- Show concept relationships

**Example:**
"While we introduce accounts receivable here with a simple example, Chapter 9 provides comprehensive coverage of receivables management, including bad debt estimation."

## Example Review Checklist

Before finalizing examples, verify:

- [ ] Example supports learning objective
- [ ] Example is appropriate complexity level
- [ ] Example maintains character/business consistency
- [ ] Example follows progressive complexity pattern
- [ ] Example includes Luxembourg context
- [ ] Example is realistic and believable
- [ ] Example is clear and easy to follow
- [ ] Example doesn't contradict previous examples
- [ ] Example builds on previous examples (if applicable)
- [ ] Example prepares for future concepts (if applicable)
