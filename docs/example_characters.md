# Example Characters and Scenarios Database

This document maintains consistency in examples throughout the Luxembourg SME Accounting Textbook by documenting recurring characters, businesses, and scenarios.

## Primary Characters and Businesses

### Marie Dubois - Le Petit Bistro

**Character Profile:**
- **Name:** Marie Dubois
- **Business:** Le Petit Bistro (restaurant)
- **Location:** Luxembourg City
- **Business Type:** Restaurant/Catering
- **Entity Type:** SARL (Société à Responsabilité Limitée)
- **Industry:** Food Service
- **Size:** Small business (SME)
- **Start Date:** Three months before Chapter 1 timeline

**Business Details:**
- **Name:** Le Petit Bistro
- **Type:** Restaurant with catering services
- **Location:** Luxembourg City center
- **Employees:** Initially just Marie, later hires staff
- **Revenue Sources:** Dine-in, takeaway, catering events
- **PCN Accounts Used:** All standard accounts for restaurant operations

**Business Evolution Timeline:**
- **Chapters 1-3:** New business, learning basics, setting up accounting
- **Chapters 4-5:** First full accounting cycle, preparing financial statements
- **Chapters 6-7:** Growing business, implementing systems, handling inventory
- **Chapters 8-10:** Established business, managing cash, receivables, inventory
- **Chapters 11-13:** Expanding, acquiring assets, managing liabilities
- **Chapters 14-17:** Mature business, considering entity changes, financial analysis

**Key Transactions (Examples):**
- Initial capital investment: €30,000
- Equipment purchases: Kitchen equipment, furniture
- Daily sales: Food and beverage sales
- Catering events: Large orders, advance payments
- Supplier purchases: Ingredients, supplies
- Employee wages: Salaries, social security
- Rent: Monthly rent payments
- VAT: All transactions include VAT

**PCN Accounts Frequently Used:**
- 510000: Cash (Caisse)
- 410000: Accounts Receivable (Créances clients)
- 400000: Accounts Payable (Dettes fournisseurs)
- 430000: VAT Payable (TVA à payer)
- 321000: Inventory (Stocks)
- 223000: Equipment (Matériel)
- 701000: Sales Revenue (Chiffre d'affaires)
- 601000: Cost of Goods Sold (Achats)
- 641000: Salaries (Salaires)
- 612000: Rent (Loyers)

**Use Cases:**
- Primary example for most chapters
- Demonstrates typical SME operations
- Shows Luxembourg-specific requirements
- Illustrates common transactions
- Represents relatable business owner

## Example Usage Guidelines

### When to Use Primary Character (Marie/Le Petit Bistro)

- **Most examples:** Primary character for consistency
- **Restaurant-specific scenarios:** Always use Marie
- **SME owner perspective:** Marie's viewpoint
- **Learning progression:** Follow Marie's learning journey
- **Common transactions:** Standard business operations

### When to Use Secondary Characters

- **Different industries:** Use industry-specific examples
- **Contrasting scenarios:** Show different business types
- **Specialized topics:** When primary character doesn't fit
- **Variety:** To avoid monotony in examples
- **Specific scenarios:** When business type matters

### When to Use Generic Examples

- **Conceptual examples:** When business type doesn't matter
- **Simple illustrations:** Basic concept demonstrations
- **Theoretical scenarios:** Abstract concepts
- **When variety needed:** Break from recurring characters

## Character Consistency Rules

### Names and Details

- **Always use full name:** "Marie Dubois" (not just "Marie" in formal contexts)
- **Consistent spelling:** Always spell names the same way
- **Consistent details:** Business details remain consistent across chapters
- **Timeline consistency:** Events follow logical timeline
- **Financial consistency:** Numbers make sense across chapters

### Business Evolution

- **Progressive growth:** Business grows logically over chapters
- **Consistent financials:** Financial numbers build on previous chapters
- **Realistic progression:** Growth is believable and gradual
- **No contradictions:** Avoid conflicting information

### Transaction Consistency

- **Account balances:** Balances carry forward logically
- **Transaction history:** Transactions build on previous examples
- **Financial statements:** Statements reflect all previous transactions
- **Realistic amounts:** All amounts are realistic for the business size
