# IFRS/GAAP Comparison for Luxembourg Context

This document provides a comparison of International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS) and U.S. Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) as they relate to Luxembourg accounting practices.

## Purpose

Luxembourg businesses may need to understand:
- When IFRS applies
- How IFRS differs from Luxembourg PCN
- How U.S. GAAP compares
- International reporting requirements
- Cross-border business considerations

## Luxembourg Accounting Standards

### PCN (Plan Comptable Normalisé)

**Status:** National accounting standard for Luxembourg
**Application:** Most SMEs in Luxembourg
**Basis:** European accounting directives
**Structure:** 7 classes of accounts

**Key Characteristics:**
- Required for most businesses
- Must file with RCS
- VAT reporting through eCDF
- French/German/English support

### When IFRS Applies

**Mandatory IFRS:**
- Listed companies on EU regulated markets
- Large companies meeting size thresholds
- Consolidated financial statements for groups

**Optional IFRS:**
- Companies may choose IFRS voluntarily
- Must apply consistently if chosen
- Cannot mix IFRS and PCN

**Luxembourg Context:**
- Most SMEs use PCN
- Larger companies may use IFRS
- International groups often use IFRS
- Must understand both for international business

## Key Differences: IFRS vs. GAAP

### 1. Inventory Valuation

**IFRS:**
- FIFO or weighted average required
- LIFO not permitted
- Lower of cost or net realizable value

**GAAP:**
- FIFO, LIFO, or weighted average allowed
- LIFO permitted (common in U.S.)
- Lower of cost or market

**Luxembourg (PCN):**
- FIFO or weighted average
- LIFO not permitted (similar to IFRS)
- Consistent with IFRS approach

### 2. Fixed Assets

**IFRS:**
- Component depreciation required
- Revaluation model allowed
- Impairment testing required

**GAAP:**
- Component depreciation not required
- Revaluation not allowed
- Impairment testing required

**Luxembourg (PCN):**
- Depreciation required
- Revaluation not common
- Generally follows cost model

### 3. Revenue Recognition

**IFRS:**
- IFRS 15 standard
- Five-step model
- Focus on transfer of control

**GAAP:**
- ASC 606 standard
- Similar five-step model
- Focus on transfer of control

**Luxembourg (PCN):**
- Revenue when earned
- Similar principles
- VAT timing considerations

### 4. Leases

**IFRS:**
- IFRS 16: Most leases on balance sheet
- Right-of-use asset model
- Single lease accounting model

**GAAP:**
- ASC 842: Most leases on balance sheet
- Right-of-use asset model
- Similar to IFRS 16

**Luxembourg (PCN):**
- Leases generally off-balance sheet
- May need to consider IFRS for international reporting

### 5. Financial Instruments

**IFRS:**
- IFRS 9: Expected credit loss model
- Fair value through profit or loss
- Amortized cost

**GAAP:**
- ASC 326: Current expected credit loss (CECL)
- Similar fair value options
- Amortized cost

**Luxembourg (PCN):**
- Bad debt provisions
- Generally simpler approach
- May need IFRS for complex instruments

- Tax considerations

## Practical Applications

### For Luxembourg SMEs

**Most SMEs:**
- Use PCN (required)
- Don't need IFRS or GAAP
- Focus on Luxembourg compliance

**Growing Businesses:**
- May need IFRS for financing
- International expansion considerations
- Investor requirements

**International Businesses:**
- May need IFRS for reporting
- Group consolidation
- Cross-border operations

### When to Consider IFRS/GAAP

**Consider IFRS If:**
- Listed on EU market
- Part of international group
- Seeking international financing
- Operating in multiple countries

**Consider GAAP If:**
- U.S. operations
- U.S. investors
- SEC reporting required
- U.S. tax considerations
